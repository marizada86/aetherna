# The Town Hall and the Council
**Category:** [[02 - Political and Urban System]]

> **Revision note.** This note used to describe a single political model for all cities. With the introduction of [[Culture System]], **the structure of government became specific to each culture**. What follows are the common rules — the skeleton every city has — and the map of where to go depending on the chosen culture.

## The Institutional Lineage

Every city passes through the same three stages. What changes is the name, the form and the rules of each.

```
Gathering Point    (Stone Age · defined by Descent)
  → Seat of government (Copper Age · defined by culture)
    → Collective body  (Bronze Age · defined by culture)
```

### Gathering Point — Stone Age

The first political structure in the game, **identical for all cultures of the same Descent**.

Five houses built close together in a habitable zone unlock its construction. Upon raising it, players choose the city's **Descent**.

Decisions are made by **in-person voting** — the orality of the Stone Age. No writing, no board, no remote voting: whoever is not physically present does not vote.

There is no formal council, office, recorded law or taxation in this era.

See [[Descent — Westerners]] · [[Descent — Easterners]] · [[Descent — Africans]] · [[Descent — Americans]].

### Collective Body — Bronze Age

Here clans become formal institutions and gain representation. Each culture has its own assembly, with distinct rules and risks.

| Culture | Body | Available forms of government |
|---|---|---|
| Norse | **Þing** | Elective chieftaincy · Hereditary chieftaincy · Council of clans · Warlord |
| Greeks | **Polis** (agora and acropolis) | Aristocracy · Oligarchy · Tyranny · Early democracy |
| Mesopotamia | Palace and council of clans | Centralized kingship · Temple government · Council of clans · Mercantile government · Military government |
| Sān Jiào | Council and bureaucracy | Bureaucratic government · Council of clans · Military government · Ritual government · Mercantile government |
| Egyptians | **Nome**, under a nomarch | Priestly government · Bureaucratic government · Aristocratic government · Military government |
| Yoruba | Council of lineages | Council of lineages · Limited kingship · Priestly government · Mercantile government |
| Aztecs | **Council of the calpulli**, under a tlatoani | Council government · Tlatoani government · Priestly government · Mercantile government |

Iron Age forms of government are listed in the `Culture — <Name>` notes.

## Writing as a Political Advantage

**In-person voting from the Stone Age is the default. Writing is what frees a city from it.**

Cultures that develop writing gain real administrative and political advantage over oral cultures. This is not flavor — it is mechanics.

| Capability | Culture with writing | Oral culture |
|---|---|---|
| Voting | **Recorded** — the vote can be cast without physical presence | **In person** — whoever is not there does not vote |
| Laws | Codified, consultable, enforceable by magistrates | Held in elders' memory, contestable |
| Taxes | Recorded by scribe; evasion is detectable | Estimated; depend on trust and prestige |
| Treaties | Verifiable document | Oath — worth the word and Honor of whoever swore it |
| Succession | Archived genealogy | Disputed by testimony |
| Subordinate territory | Administrable at a distance by record | Requires presence, emissary or visit |

### What Oral Cultures Gain in Return

Yoruba and Tupi-Guarani are not simply worse off. The absence of written record gives them:

* **Immunity to record destruction.** There is no codex to burn and no stela to topple. The wraths of Bragi, Itzamná and Thoth, which erase records, have no equivalent target.
* **Nothing to intercept.** [[Languages and Letter Security|The Language Barrier]] protects stolen letters, but an oral city **produces no letters** to be stolen in the first place.
* **Zero administrative cost.** They need not sustain scribes nor suffer the bureaucratic backlog that punishes Mesopotamia and Sān Jiào when records exceed officials.
* **Mediation instead of judgment.** Systems like Yoruba Àṣẹ and Tupi-Guarani Teko reward resolving disputes without violence, which a law-oriented city cannot do.

The real trade is **reach for resilience**: writing governs far, orality cannot be erased.

## What Is Common to All Cultures

Regardless of culture, the city's governing body decides:

1. The rate of [[The Tax and Gold System]].
2. The advancement of **Eras**, unlocking new gathering and production tiers — see [[Era Progression]] and [[Gathering and Minigames System]].
3. The **patron deity** at every era transition, from Copper onward — see [[Deities and Passives]].
4. Declaration of war against neighboring cities or factions of [[Bandits and Full Loot]].

### Expropriation and Control

The governing body holds the Expropriation tool. It can vote to seize a citizen's standard plot, whether to widen streets or expel internal enemies. The only defense against this power is owning property in the [[Urban Zoning and Elite Zones]].

## The Town Hall as Founding Structure

The original model — the Town Hall raised by a group of players gathering a massive quantity of basic resources — **describes the leap into the Copper Age**, not the city's initial founding.

The real founding is far cheaper: five houses and a Gathering Point. The heavy investment happens when the community decides to become a city proper, build a temple and choose a deity.

## Switching Culture Changes the Government

The structure of government belongs to the culture. **Switching culture at an era transition brings the government down with it.**

A Norse city that starts following a Greek god loses the þing and adopts the Greek structure. Offices of the previous culture cease to exist; the new form of government is chosen from the destination culture's options.

**Consequences:**

* **Offices are abolished.** Jarls, archons, nomarchs, tlatoanis and ajaws exist only in their cultures. Whoever held an office loses the position.
* **Laws and treaties recorded under the previous structure** must be revalidated by the new body. Under Tyr's wrath, they simply fall.
* **The city pays twice:** the Wrath of the Abandoned God for 24 hours **and** the institutional vacuum while the new government has not formed.
* **An oral culture that becomes a writing culture** gains the administrative advantage described above — and the reverse also holds: a city that abandons writing **loses access to what it had already recorded**.

This makes switching culture a decision of political refounding, not a bonus adjustment. The Wrath lasts 24 hours; the reorganization of government lasts as long as the city takes to reorganize.

## Open Questions

* Whether the Gathering Point can be destroyed, and what happens to a Descent already chosen.
* Quorum for in-person voting in the Stone Age: majority of those present, majority of citizens, or consensus of the clans.
* Reconciling each culture's **council of clans** with the [[The Council]] elected among players described in the rest of the GDD. Are they the same body under different names?
* Who decides the culture switch, if it brings down the very body that decided it. Would a council vote for its own abolition?
* Whether the city is left without a functioning government during the transition, and for how long.
