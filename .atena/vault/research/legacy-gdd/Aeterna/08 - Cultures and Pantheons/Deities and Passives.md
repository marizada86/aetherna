# Deities and Passives

**Category:** [[08 - Cultures and Pantheons]]

**81 deities**: 9 per culture, 3 per unlockable era. The Stone Age has none — there is no religion before Copper.

The patron deity grants a **permanent passive** to the city while the cult remains active. Individual passives are in the `Pantheon — <Culture>` notes — see [[Culture System]].

## Deities Are Era-Locked

**A god exists only in its own era.** Freyr is accessible in Copper and only in Copper. On advancing to Bronze, Freyr becomes unavailable — not as patron, not as secondary cult, not in any form.

| Era | Deities | Ult scale |
|---|---|---|
| Stone | **None** | — |
| Copper | 3 per culture | Single target, no AoE |
| Bronze | 3 per culture | AoE up to 3×3 |
| Iron | 3 per culture | AoE up to 5×5 |

**Direct consequence: every era transition forces a change of deity.** The city does not choose *whether* to change patron — only *whom* to change to.

> This replaces the former rule that "deities from previous eras remain available" and eliminates the secondary cult system at 25%. A god of a past era cannot be worshipped in any form.

## Choice Moments

| Transition | What happens |
|---|---|
| Stone → Copper | First deity, among the three of Copper. **Defines the city's culture** |
| Copper → Bronze | Mandatory choice among the three of Bronze |
| Bronze → Iron | Mandatory choice among the three of Iron |

Within a single era the deity does not change. The choice exists only at the transition.

## Cost of Switching

| Situation | Allowed | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| New deity, **same culture** | Yes | **None.** It is the city's natural progression |
| New deity, **another culture of the same Descent** | Yes | **Wrath of the Abandoned God** — 24 hours |
| New deity, **another Descent** | No | — |

Switching culture is blasphemy: the abandoned god grows enraged and punishes the city.

## Wrath of the Abandoned God

**The punishment is the exact inverse of the abandoned god's passive.** If the passive granted agricultural production, the wrath makes fields lose fertility. If it granted Order conditioned on honored treaties, the wrath annuls the treaties.

**Duration: 24 hours.** Temporary by design — a permanent wrath would make switching culture a dead decision.

Generating rule: to write any deity's wrath, take each line of the passive and flip its sign, preserving the theme.

| The passive grants | The wrath takes |
|---|---|
| Percentage bonus to X | Penalty to X, at **double the magnitude** |
| Favorable thematic effect | The same effect, turned against the city |
| Condition that amplifies the bonus | The same condition, now worsening the penalty |
| Requirement of the cult | **The requirement remains**, without the benefit |

**Magnitude: double the passive, inverted.** A Copper god that gave +5% applies −10%. A Bronze god that gave +10% applies −20%. An Iron god that gave +15% applies −30%.

### Severity by Era

| Era of the abandoned god | Reach of the wrath |
|---|---|
| Copper | **Production** — the resource or profession it favored |
| Bronze | **Structure and relations** — buildings, formations, clan loyalty, treaties |
| Iron | **Accumulated assets** — research, prestige, records, population, memory of the run |

Betraying an Iron god costs far more than betraying a Copper one. This makes switching culture cheap early and expensive late, encouraging the city to commit to an identity as it advances.

Individual wraths are listed in the `Pantheon — <Culture>` notes.

## Passive Power Structure

| Era | Expected power | Passive structure |
|---|---|---|
| Copper | Basic | One main bonus of ~5% and a small thematic advantage |
| Bronze | Intermediate | A bonus of ~10% or two complementary effects |
| Iron | Advanced | A bonus of ~15%, accompanied by a strategic rule |

## System Terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Production** | Amount of resources generated by workers and buildings |
| **Construction speed** | Time required to complete buildings |
| **Order** | Stability, compliance with laws and resistance to revolt |
| **Satisfaction** | Contentment of the citizens |
| **Faith** | Resource produced by temples, priests, festivals and offerings. **Does not exist in the Stone Age** |
| **Prestige** | Reputation of the city before clans, visitors and other cities |
| **Health** | Reduces disease, mortality and recovery time |
| **Population growth** | Speed at which new citizens are born, migrate or join the city |
| **Military strength** | General modifier applied to military units |
| **Urban defense** | Modifier applied to walls, towers, gates and defenders in the city's territory |

## Balancing Rules

### 1. A passive must not cover everything

Each god strengthens a specific strategy. Demeter strengthens agriculture; Hephaestus, metallurgy; Ares, attacks; Athena, strategy and defense; Hades, mining and funerary administration.

### 2. Broad passives must carry conditions

A god granting bonuses across several areas must demand a condition or present a risk. Loki offers opportunities but creates unpredictable results; Set improves adaptation to chaos but reduces stability; Tau increases wealth but also corruption and rivalry; Huitzilopochtli requires military activity to sustain fervor.

### 3. The three deities of an era must be distinct from one another

Because the era locks access, the player chooses among exactly three options per transition. They must point to clearly different strategies, with no dominant one.

### 4. The Copper choice is the heaviest

It defines the city's culture. Later choices swap the passive; the first swaps the entire game.

### 5. Switching culture must be tempting, not overly punishing

The Wrath lasts 24 hours and destroys no permanent progress. The goal is a real dilemma: is a superior Bronze passive worth 24 hours of wrath from the abandoned Copper god?

## Relation to the Rest of the GDD

The temple is the economy's main systemic sink per [[The Tax and Gold System]]. Here it gains its second function: choosing the patron and activating the passive. The **regular offerings** demanded by several Iron Age deities are the concrete mechanism of that drain.

The choice of patron also determines which Divine Ults are available — see [[Divine Ult Rules]] and [[3-Ability Combat System]].
