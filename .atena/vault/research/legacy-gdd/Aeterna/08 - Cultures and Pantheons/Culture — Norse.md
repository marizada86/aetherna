# Culture — Norse

**Category:** [[08 - Cultures and Pantheons]] · **Descent:** Westerners · **Pantheon:** [[Pantheon — Norse]]

Six pillars: clans and lineages · honor and reputation · community assemblies · seafaring · expeditions · oaths and loyalty.

The city begins as a small community of families and evolves into a regional dominion sustained by halls, assemblies, ports, war chiefs and networks of subordinate settlements.

| Era | Norse stage | Population | Main advance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stone | Western Settlement | 20 | Descent chosen at the Gathering Point · no religion |
| Copper | Hall Settlement | 50 | Temple, great hall, professions and patron god |
| Bronze | City of the Þing | 100 | Clan seats, formal assembly, regional trade and standing army |
| Iron | Norse Dominion | 150 | Districts, jarls, fleets, colonies and territorial expansion |

## Identity

**Strengths:** cohesive clans · military morale · seafaring · exploration · combat in difficult terrain · timber production · animal husbandry · long-distance expeditions · climatic resilience · communal defense.

**Weaknesses:** rivalries between lineages · family vendettas · succession disputes · limited agricultural output · dependence on sea routes · risk of political fragmentation · constant need to maintain honor and Prestige · difficulty centralizing power.

**Play style.** Resilient communities, coastal settlements, a mixed economy of agriculture, herding, fishing and trade, maritime expeditions, militarily relevant clans, governments based on oaths and personal recognition, war. Norse power depends on Honor, Prestige, loyalty, fulfilled oaths, clan support, military victories and **the ruler's generosity**.

## Stone Age — defined by Descent

This culture's Stone Age **does not exist separately**. It belongs to the Westerners Descent and is identical for all its cultures.

See **[[Descent — Westerners]]** for the full package: founding via the Gathering Point, social organization, government by in-person voting, buildings, units and cultural trait.

Norse identity begins only in the **Copper Age**, with the choice of the first deity. There is no religion in the Stone Age.

## Copper Age — Hall Settlement (50)

Specialized farmers, smiths, carpenters, boat builders, merchants, permanent warriors, priests and animal breeders emerge. **The political and social center becomes the great hall.**

**Available patron gods:** Freyr · Uller · Bragi.

**What activates with the culture:**
- **Great Hall** replaces the Gathering Point as political center. It becomes tribunal, religious space, feast venue, gift-distribution center and reception hall for foreigners.
- **Honor system** — important citizens accumulate Honor. It rises by keeping promises, defending the community, sharing resources, winning battles. It falls by fleeing without need, abandoning allies, breaking oaths, stealing from another clan.
- **Gift system** — the ruler distributes weapons, jewels, animals, offices and spoils. Raises loyalty, Honor and Prestige; poorly distributed, it breeds jealousy and accusations of favoritism.
- **Jarls** — clan leaders become recognized as jarls. A chief performing poorly may be **challenged by a jarl** for leadership.
- **Guardian of Oaths** as an office.

**Government:** hall chieftain supported by a local council. Institutions: great hall, settlement chief, council of elders, limited community assembly, resource administrator, guardian of oaths.

**Economy:** copper, timber, fish, wool, leather, grain, meat, honey, hides, **amber**, stone. Production: mead, wool textiles, copper tools and weapons, shields, coastal boats, tanned hides, carved objects, simple jewelry, salted fish.

**New buildings:** simple longhouse · pasture, smokehouse, food preservation house · copper mine and smelter, smithy, shipyard, carving workshop, **brewery or mead house** · great hall, council house, assembly square · temple, priests' house, shrine, preserved sacred grove · rudimentary port, dock, coastal trading post · wooden wall, coastal post.

**Military:** small standing guard — spearman with shield, axe warrior, archer, hunter, hall guard, armed sailor, scout, hunting-spear warrior.

### Cultural trait — Hospitality of the Hall

The great hall is simultaneously political center, feast venue, informal tribunal, religious space, gift-distribution center and reception hall for foreigners.

- **Benefits:** administrative, religious and commercial buildings near the hall receive more Prestige, loyalty, efficiency and morale recovery.
- **Risks:** lack of food, gifts or festivals causes loss of Prestige, clan dissatisfaction, reduced loyalty and **challenges to leadership**.

**Missions:** build the great hall · raise the temple · organize the crafts · form the guard · build the port · conduct a trading expedition · mediate the clans · celebrate the winter · discover bronze.

**Advancing:** great hall · temple · patron god · market · port · specialized professions · standing guard · bronze · 50 citizens · clan conflicts controlled · clans ready for seats.

## Bronze Age — City of the Þing (100)

The city organizes around the **þing**, the formal assembly. Clans become political institutions, seats and clan missions unlock, recognized laws appear, along with a standing army, regional trade and larger naval expeditions.

**New gods:** Thor · Tyr · Loki. Those of Copper **expire** on turning the era — the choice among the three of Bronze is mandatory. Switching to another culture of the same Descent triggers the [[Deities and Passives|Wrath of the Abandoned God]].

### Citizenship and Free Status

**Free citizens may:** take part in the þing · witness oaths · own land · serve in the army · receive legal compensation · back chieftains.

**They must:** defend the city · take part in public works · comply with þing decisions · pay tribute · honor oaths.

### Clans

Norse types: agricultural · pastoral · mercantile · maritime · warrior · priestly · **smith** · **shipwright** · hunter · aristocratic · integrated foreign. See [[Clan System]].

Unlocks include **representation in the þing**.

### The Þing

Decides laws, disputes, compensation, punishments, recognition of chieftains, declarations of war, treaties and property rights.

### Forms of Government

| Form | Benefits | Risks |
|---|---|---|
| **Elective chieftaincy** — the chief depends on þing support | Loyalty of free citizens · lower risk of tyranny · better dispute resolution | Slow decisions · unstable leadership · electoral disputes |
| **Hereditary chieftaincy** — power stays in the lineage | Predictable succession · greater central authority · administrative continuity | Incapable heirs · rivalry between lineages · succession crises |
| **Council of clans** — the main clans share power | Support of powerful families · better funding of works · stability among elites | Smaller clans dissatisfied · political paralysis · rival blocs |
| **Warlord** — a military chief dominates | Fast mobilization · stronger troops · efficient expeditions | Instability · rebellions · dependence on victories |

**Economy:** bronze weapons, shields, armor, mead, salted fish, naval timber, ships, hides, amber, wool textiles, jewelry, carved objects, farming tools.

**Buildings:** expanded great hall, **þing field**, house of laws, treasury, tribunal, **archive of oaths**, messengers' house · clan seat, maritime clan dock, pastoral clan pen · bronze smelter, great smithy, shipyard, jewelry and carving workshops · great market, commercial port, port district · stone wall, military shipyard, harbor fortress · **skalds' hall**, **rune stone**, games field, banquet hall.

**Military:** shield warrior, spearman, axe warrior, archer, veteran hunter, **early huscarl**, scout, war sailor, hall guard, naval engineer, rudimentary siege unit.

### Signature formation — Shield Wall

Adjacent warriors receive frontal defense, resistance to projectiles, resistance to knockback and additional morale.

Loses efficiency when flanked, surrounded, broken by heavy units, moved quickly or used on very uneven terrain.

### Cultural trait — Recognized Oaths

Oaths gain political and legal weight. They may cover alliances, marriages, military service, protection, trade, vassalage, peace and compensation.

**Keeping them grants:** Honor · Prestige · loyalty · diplomatic trust.
**Breaking them causes:** loss of Honor · clan hostility · religious penalties · **casus belli** · revolts.

**Missions:** found the þing · recognize the clans · record the laws · form the shield wall · expand the port · raise a great temple · balance the clans · conduct an expedition · create a public treasury · master iron.

Clan missions include a type unique to the culture: **skald clan** — compose a saga, record a victory, honor a hero, restore a family's reputation.

**Advancing:** 100 citizens · clan seats · functioning þing · clan missions · avoid internal war · stone walls · standing army · trade route · regional port · great temple · iron metallurgy · **at least one successful expedition**.

## Iron Age — Norse Dominion (150)

The city becomes the center of a regional dominion over villages, farms, ports, forts, colonies, islands, trade routes, conquered territories and tributary settlements. It requires balancing ruler, jarls, þing, clan chieftains, priests, merchants, warriors, navigators, skalds and subordinate populations.

**New gods:** Odin · Freya · Balder. Those of Bronze **expire** on turning the era — the choice among the three of Iron is mandatory. Switching to another culture of the same Descent triggers the [[Deities and Passives|Wrath of the Abandoned God]].

### Forms of Government

| Form | Benefits | Risks |
|---|---|---|
| **Elective kingship** | Legitimacy before the þing · balance among clans · lower risk of incapable heirs | Electoral crises · vote buying · disputes among candidates |
| **Hereditary kingship** | Political continuity · centralization · stable administration | Succession disputes · weak heirs · jarl rebellions |
| **Confederation of jarls** | Regional military capacity · decentralized administration · fast border response | Low centralization · rival jarls · secessions · internal wars |
| **Þing government** | Satisfaction of free citizens · legitimate laws · lower risk of tyranny | Slow decisions · political deadlock · difficulty in prolonged campaigns |
| **Military lordship** | Fast campaigns · greater recruitment · efficient expansion | Instability · dependence on victories · revolts after defeats |

**Economy:** currency, taxation, customs, settlement tribute, naval trade, expeditions, colonies, river routes, regional markets, **distribution of spoils**.

**Buildings:** palace or fortified great hall, regional þing, house of laws, archive of oaths, royal treasury, mint, embassy, **jarls' hall** · expanded seat, fortified hall, clan fortress, clan dock, lineage archive, manorial farm · iron mine, primitive blast furnace, great forge, advanced shipyard · naval and mercantile ports, customs, credit house, naval warehouse · fortified wall, citadel, military academy, coastal and border forts · cistern, house of healing, **heated bathhouse** · **rune school**, **saga library**, cartographers' workshop, **navigation academy**, naval engineering workshop.

**Military:** sword warrior, veteran spearman, axe warrior, veteran archer, **huscarl**, **berserker**, jarl's guard, scout, war sailor, engineer, siege unit, commander, urban guard. **Naval:** merchant boat, exploration ship, **longship**, transport ship, heavy warship, colonization vessel, patrol ship.

### Cultural trait — Fame Beyond the Sea

The city gains Prestige by discovering new lands, winning battles, founding colonies, establishing routes, bringing back treasure, keeping oaths and composing sagas.

The player may declare a **regional vocation** — commercial, naval, military, colonial, religious, cultural or diplomatic. Specialization grants relevant bonuses but increases rivalries, clan expectations, upkeep costs and risk of external conflict.

### Saga System

Important events can be recorded as sagas: the founding of the city, great battles, expeditions, heroic deaths, alliances, betrayals, monumental constructions, conquests, disasters.

**Sagas grant:** Prestige · morale · legitimacy · cultural identity · **bonuses to descendants**.

**Negative sagas perpetuate:** vendettas · humiliations · rivalries · territorial claims.

### Colonization and Expeditions

**Foundable settlements:** rural villages, ports, colonies, forts, trading posts, island settlements.

**Expedition types:** commercial · exploratory · diplomatic · colonial · military · religious · monster hunt · relic search.

**Required preparation:** ships · crew · food · water · weapons · tools · navigators · a leader · clan support.

**Missions:** reform the þing · recognize the jarls · conduct the census · build a great fleet · found a colony · create a rune school · celebrate a great festival · resolve a clan crisis · lead a confederation · establish regional supremacy.

**Era risks:** civil war · jarl rebellion · succession crisis · broken oaths · vendetta between clans · naval blockade · colonial rebellion · famine · harsh winter · epidemics · piracy · invasions · corruption · territorial fragmentation · usurpation.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Urban form | Western Settlement | Hall settlement | City of the þing | Regional dominion |
| Government | Chief and elders | Hall chieftain | Þing and clan chieftains | King, jarls or regional þing |
| Clans | Informal | Economic and religious | Institutionalized | Houses, jarls and factions |
| Religion | **None** | Temple patron | Urban cult | Regional religious system |
| Urban center | Gathering Point | Great hall | Þing and port | Districts, palace and naval port |
| Military | Communal defense | Local guard | Army and shield wall | Regional army and fleet |

| Expansion | Survival | Coastal navigation | Regional expeditions | Colonies and territorial dominion |

**Identity of each era:** Stone, Westerners Descent (see [[Descent — Westerners]]) · Copper, hall and hospitality · Bronze, þing and oaths · Iron, fleets and dominion.
