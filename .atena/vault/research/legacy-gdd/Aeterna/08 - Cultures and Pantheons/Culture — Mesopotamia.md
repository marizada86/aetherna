# Culture — Mesopotamia

**Category:** [[08 - Cultures and Pantheons]] · **Descent:** Easterners · **Pantheon:** [[Pantheon — Mesopotamia]]

Seven pillars: irrigation · temples · granaries · writing · trade · laws · urban power.

The city begins as an agricultural community dependent on rivers and canals and becomes a bureaucratic, walled city-state capable of controlling territories, trade routes and subordinate cities.

| Era | Mesopotamian stage | Population | Main advance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stone | Eastern Settlement | 20 | Descent chosen at the Gathering Point · no religion |
| Copper | Temple Settlement | 50 | Organized irrigation, temple, market and patron god |
| Bronze | Walled City-State | 100 | Clan seats, writing, laws, army and regional trade |
| Iron | Imperial Capital | 150 (capital) | Districts, provinces, great works, diplomacy and expansion |

## Identity

**Strengths:** irrigated agriculture · surplus production · public storage · administrative writing · taxation · long-distance trade · monumental construction · laws · diplomacy · territorial administration · pottery and metallurgy · urban growth.

**Weaknesses:** dependence on the rivers · vulnerability to floods and droughts · **soil salinization** · high administrative cost · bureaucratic corruption · rivalry between temples and palace · rebellions of subordinate cities · need to import timber, stone and metals · conflicts between mercantile, priestly and military clans · difficulty maintaining large canals.

**Play style.** Dense, walled, irrigated, bureaucratic, specialized cities connected by rivers and caravans. Power depends on control of the waters, agricultural surplus, records, laws, religious legitimacy, trade, tribute, military capacity and monumental prestige.

## Core cultural system — Order of the City

A value exclusive to the culture, representing the functioning of the canals, compliance with the laws, provisioning, administrative efficiency, religious legitimacy, security and trust in public records.

**Rises by:** maintaining canals · storing food · recording resources · complying with laws · resolving disputes · paying workers · protecting routes · maintaining temples · avoiding corruption · controlling prices in crises · keeping subordinate cities loyal.

**Falls with:** famine · flood · drought · salinization · corruption · falsified records · revolt · civil war · abandonment of temples · breach of contracts · granary theft · canal failure.

| High Order | Low Order |
|---|---|
| Greater productivity · better revenue · less waste · more loyalty · better trade · lower crime risk · more efficient public buildings · more Prestige | Unstable market · resource diversion · famine · crime · clan conflicts · revolts · lower production · loss of legitimacy · less loyal subordinate cities |

## Environmental system — Rivers and Irrigation

Production depends on a network of canals, dikes, reservoirs, sluices, irrigated fields and drainage ditches. **The network requires permanent maintenance.**

**Benefits:** greater production · agriculture in dry areas · population growth · river transport · reduced drought impact · more taxes.

**Problems:** silting · dike breaches · floods · standing water · disease · **salinization** · disputes between districts · sabotage.

Continuously irrigated fields suffer salinization. Mitigations: rotate crops · create drainage · rest fields · open new areas · control water volume.

## Stone Age — defined by Descent

This culture's Stone Age **does not exist separately**. It belongs to the Easterners Descent and is identical for all its cultures.

See **[[Descent — Easterners]]** for the full package: founding via the Gathering Point, social organization, government by in-person voting, buildings, units and cultural trait.

Mesopotamian identity begins only in the **Copper Age**, with the choice of the first deity. There is no religion in the Stone Age.

## Copper Age — Temple Settlement (50)

The urban center forms around temple, granary, market, square and the ruler's house.

**Available patron gods:** Dumuzi · Ninurta · Utu/Shamash.

**Government:** urban chief or **priest-ruler** supported by a local council. Institutions: ruler's house, local council, granary administrator, **canal supervisor**, market administrator, guard captain, priestly council. Authority depends on control of food, canal maintenance, religious support, security, prosperity and **Order of the City**.

**Economy:** copper, grain, dates, wool, fish, clay, reeds, leather, bitumen, **imported** timber and stone, herbs, milk, beer. Production: copper tools, pottery, wool textiles, bricks, river boats, simple jewelry, beer, bread, religious objects, **rudimentary seals**.

**New buildings:** adobe house · irrigated field, date orchard, bakery, brewery · copper mine and smelter, brick workshop, **seal workshop**, river shipyard · **irrigation canal, dike, reservoir, rudimentary drainage**, dock, simple bridge · ruler's house, **public granary**, canal supervisor's house · temple, **temple warehouse**, ancestral chapel · local market, caravan house · adobe and wood wall, river post.

**Military:** small standing guard — spearman with shield, archer, slinger, temple guard, **river patroller**, scout, axe warrior, armed sailor.

### Cultural trait — Temple Economy

The temple can store food, employ workers, distribute seed, fund construction, organize festivals and **grant loans in goods**.

- **Benefits:** more stability · better famine resistance · more Faith · greater public works capacity.
- **Risks:** priests accumulate power · **the ruler loses authority** · agricultural clans become dependent · corruption and resource diversion.

### Early Contract System

Agreements recorded by symbols, seals or witnesses, covering exchanges, loans, land, labor, marriages and compensation.

Honoring them raises Order, Prestige and commercial trust. Breaking them causes disputes, loss of Prestige, penalties and rivalry between clans.

**Missions:** raise the first temple · control the canals · organize the granary · form the market · organize the crafts · form the guard · **create contracts** · mediate the clans · open a river route · discover bronze.

**Advancing:** temple · patron god · maintained canals · granary · market · professions · standing guard · **contract system** · bronze · 50 citizens · controlled conflicts · clans ready for seats · **stable Order**.

## Bronze Age — Walled City-State (100)

Cuneiform writing, scribes, clan seats, clan missions, recorded laws, taxation, palace, ziggurat, standing army, regional trade and inter-city diplomacy emerge.

**New gods:** Nergal/Erra · Inanna/Ishtar · Gibil/Nusku. Those of Copper **expire** on turning the era — the choice among the three of Bronze is mandatory. Switching to another culture of the same Descent triggers the [[Deities and Passives|Wrath of the Abandoned God]].

### Clans

Mesopotamian types: agricultural · pastoral · mercantile · priestly · **scribal** · military · potter · builder · metallurgical · **river** · aristocratic · integrated foreign. See [[Clan System]].

Unlocks include **canal funding** and **participation in tribunals**.

### Forms of Government

| Form | Benefits | Risks |
|---|---|---|
| **Centralized kingship** | Fast construction · coordinated army · efficient taxation · better crisis response | Coups · revolts · succession crises · rivalry with priests |
| **Temple government** | High religious stability · efficient granaries · productive temples · better response to food crises | High cost of offerings · overly powerful priesthood · conflict with merchants and military |
| **Council of clans** | Elite support · more private investment · better internal negotiation | Slow decisions · rivalries · smaller clans excluded |
| **Mercantile government** | More trade · expanded routes · better access to imports · greater revenue | Corruption · monopolies · inequality · external dependence |
| **Military government** | Strong army · fast campaigns · better route defense | High cost · coups · less civil investment · dependence on victories |

### Cuneiform Writing

Allows recording tribute, contracts, land, laws, trade, families, debts, armies, harvests and historical events.

**Benefits:** less waste · better revenue · more research · better diplomacy · fewer disputes · more Order.

### Laws

Covering property, trade, debt, marriage, inheritance, labor, crime, **irrigation**, tribute and military service.

Clear laws raise Order, Prestige, security and commercial trust. Unjust or contradictory laws provoke revolts, corruption, citizen flight and rivalries.

**Buildings:** palace, council of clans, tribunal, treasury, archive, **house of scribes**, collection post, messengers' house · clan seat, records house, private dock · bronze smelter, **main canal**, great granary, seal workshop, **tablet workshop**, shipyard · great market, river port, caravan house, customs post · reinforced adobe wall, river fort · school of scribes, **house of tablets**, school of mathematics, **early observatory**, house of medicine.

**Military:** spearman, archer, slinger, shield warrior, palace guard, temple guard, scout, war sailor, **early chariot**, siege engineer, officer.

### Signature formation — Shield Line

Adjacent units gain frontal defense, resistance to projectiles, morale and better control by officers.

Loses efficiency when flanked, surrounded, moved over bad terrain or **separated from the commander**.

### Cultural trait — City of Tablets

Each administrative sector can generate records. The more correct records exist, the better taxation, the lower corruption, the more efficient the canals, the better trade and the faster research.

**An excess of records without enough scribes** causes delays, errors, bureaucratic backlog and resource loss.

**Missions:** found the city-state · recognize the clans · create the house of scribes · record the laws · **build the ziggurat** · control irrigation · form a standing army · open a regional route · create a public archive · master iron.

**Advancing:** 100 citizens · clan seats · functioning government · clan missions · writing and laws · maintained canals · **controlled salinization** · walls · standing army · trade route · ziggurat · iron · avoid internal war · high Order · **subordinate or ally with another settlement**.

## Iron Age — Imperial Capital (150 in the capital)

> Subordinate cities and villages have their own populations and are administered separately.

The capital governs villages, fields, canals, mines, ports, forts, subordinate cities, provinces, trade routes and conquered territories.

**New gods:** Marduk · Enki/Ea · Ereshkigal. Those of Bronze **expire** on turning the era — the choice among the three of Iron is mandatory. Switching to another culture of the same Descent triggers the [[Deities and Passives|Wrath of the Abandoned God]].

### Forms of Government

| Form | Benefits | Risks |
|---|---|---|
| **Centralized monarchy** | Great construction capacity · coordinated army · efficient taxation · better provincial control | Coups · succession crises · rebellions · high administrative cost |
| **Priestly monarchy** | High legitimacy · religious stability · efficient temples · better population control | Overly powerful priests · conflict with generals · high cost of offerings |
| **Provincial administration** | Better local response · lower central cost · more productive provinces | Rebel governors · withheld tribute · provincial dynasties · fragmentation |
| **Military government** | Strong army · fast expansion · better border control | Coups · constant campaigns · high cost · less civil investment |
| **Mercantile government** | More trade · better revenue · expanded diplomacy · greater resource access | Corruption · monopolies · inequality · external dependence |

**Economy:** mint, imperial taxation, customs, credit, loans, **interest**, state warehouses, river routes, caravans, provincial tribute, food distribution, monopolies.

**Buildings:** fortified palace, throne room, higher council, royal archive, mint, **ministry of canals**, **ministry of tribute**, governors' hall · expanded seat, clan palace, fortress, private dock · iron mine, primitive blast furnace, **advanced irrigation**, water mill, textile complex, advanced shipyard · great market, river and military ports, credit house, royal warehouse · fortified wall, citadel, river forts · **aqueduct or elevated canal**, cisterns, drainage, rudimentary public lighting · **great school of scribes**, **library of tablets**, observatory, school of mathematics, school of medicine, cartographic workshop, palace garden.

**Military:** iron spearman, veteran archer, veteran slinger, shield warrior, royal guard, temple guard, **chariot** and elite chariot, scout, war sailor, engineer, siege unit, general, urban guard.

**Possible special units:** Guard of Marduk · War priest · Engineer of Enki · **Funerary guard of Ereshkigal** · Royal chariot · Military scribe.

### Signature formation — Imperial Line

Units under officers gain frontal defense, morale, better discipline, resistance to disorganization and greater logistical efficiency.

Loses efficiency when officers are defeated, lines are flanked, the army runs out of supplies or **communication is cut**.

### Cultural trait — Network of Provinces

The capital administers provinces and subordinate cities. Each territory can specialize in agriculture, herding, mining, trade, religion, war, craftsmanship or navigation.

**High loyalty:** more tribute · more available soldiers · more special resources · better trade.
**Dissatisfied provinces may:** withhold tribute · falsify records · back pretenders · refuse troops · form alliances · **rebel**.

### Legal Code System

The code may prioritize egalitarian justice, royal authority, property protection, trade, agriculture, religion or military power. Each priority grants bonuses and penalties.

Laws must be recorded, published, enforced and interpreted by judges. **Uneven enforcement** causes corruption, revolts, loss of Order and class rivalry.

### Debt System

Citizens and clans may take on debt to finance planting, trade, workshops, construction and expeditions.

**Excessive debt causes:** loss of land · forced labor · revolts · wealth concentration · cancellation or reform missions.

The ruler may declare **debt forgiveness** to restore stability, raise loyalty and reduce creditor power — at the cost of lost mercantile trust, capital flight and rivalry with commercial clans.

### Monumentality System

Ziggurats, palaces, monumental gates, walls, statues, gardens, canals and ceremonial avenues grant Prestige, legitimacy, Faith, immigration and regional influence.

**Excessive projects cause:** worker shortages · stock depletion · debt · revolts · **abandoned canals**.

**Missions:** conduct the census · reform the provinces · modernize the canals · create an imperial code · build a monumental ziggurat · found a citadel · administer the districts · standardize currency and measures · create a diplomatic network · resolve a clan crisis · **resolve a succession crisis** · **suppress or negotiate a provincial revolt** · establish regional supremacy.

**Era risks:** destructive flood · drought · salinization · famine · epidemic · corruption · falsified records · provincial revolt · coup · succession crisis · civil war · debt · conflict between temple and palace · trade blockade · invasion · **canal collapse** · abandoned monumental work · loss of Order.

| Urban form | Eastern Settlement | Temple settlement | Walled city-state | Imperial capital |
| Government | Chief and elders | Priest-ruler | King, temple or council | Imperial monarchy or provincial administration |
| Religion | **None** | Temple patron | Ziggurat and urban cult | Imperial religious network |
| Agriculture | Initial cultivation | Organized irrigation | Canal network | Regional irrigation and salinization control |
| Military | Communal defense | Local guard | Standing army | Imperial army and chariots |

**Identity of each era:** Stone, Easterners Descent (see [[Descent — Easterners]]) · Copper, temple and granary · Bronze, writing and city-state · Iron, laws and empire.
