# Descent — Americans

**Category:** [[08 - Cultures and Pantheons]] · **Cultures:** [[Culture — Aztecs]] · [[Culture — Maya]] · [[Culture — Tupi-Guarani]]

**Stone Age** package for every city of American Descent. Identical for whoever later becomes Aztec, Maya or Tupi-Guarani. It is the only Descent with **three** cultures.

See [[Culture System]] and [[Era Progression]].

## Stone Age — American Settlement

**Maximum population:** 20 citizens.

### Identity

A community of extended families and lineages settled among forest, cropland and watercourse. The population depends on rudimentary swidden fields, hunting, fishing, gathering, early animal husbandry, timber extraction, herbs and direct barter.

There is no ceremonial center, writing, formal calendar, organized market or urban government.

### Founding

Five houses built close together in a habitable zone unlock the **Gathering Point**. On raising it, players choose the Descent and the city becomes American.

### Social Organization

Extended families · lineages · informal clans · elders · rudimentary farmers · hunters · fishers · gatherers · artisans · healers · communal warriors.

Clans influence marriages, professions, food distribution, land use, support for the chief, defense, funerary rites, productive cooperation and family rivalries.

### Government

Community leadership decided at the Gathering Point by **in-person voting**.

**Institutions:** community leader or chief · informal council of elders · circle of families · **guardian of the seeds** · **observer of the rains** · clan spokespeople.

Decisions rest on prestige, age, leadership capacity, agricultural production, **knowledge of the territory** and family support.

### Religion

**None.** No temple, priesthood, patron deity, Faith or religious buildings. Burials are a social custom with no mechanical effect.

### Economy

Direct barter.

**Resources:** maize · beans · squash · cassava · sweet potato · chili peppers · fruit · fish · meat · timber · stone · **obsidian or flint, depending on the region** · clay · fiber · herbs · honey · hides.

**Production:** stone tools · **volcanic-glass blades** · baskets · rope · fishing nets · rudimentary pottery · simple textiles · wood and stone weapons · small canoes · dried food · herbal remedies.

### Buildings

**Housing:** family hut · communal house · hunters' shelter · fishers' hut · **family courtyard** · temporary camp.

**Food:** rudimentary swidden field · primitive garden · gathering field · fishing spot · hunting camp · primitive granary · food drying area · tuber processing area.

**Production:** stone workshop · **volcanic-glass workshop** · bone workshop · rudimentary pottery · weaving area · basket workshop · herb preparation area · communal fire.

**Infrastructure:** shallow well · dirt path · **forest trail** · natural reservoir · communal warehouse · water distribution point · rudimentary wooden walkway.

**Administration:** **Gathering Point** · leader's house · circle of elders · **community courtyard** · territorial marker.

**Defense:** palisade · barrier of wood and thorns · watchpost · **observation platform** · rudimentary tower · simple gate · warriors' shelter.

### Military Forces

Temporarily levied citizens. There is no standing army.

Hunter · tribal spearman · slinger · rudimentary archer · warrior with club · **warrior with volcanic-glass blade** · forest scout · armed fisher · defender with wooden shield.

### Cultural trait — Intercropped Cultivation

Maize, beans and squash can be grown together in the same field. Where cassava is the staple, the intercrop organizes around it.

**Benefits:** greater food variety · better soil fertility · **lower risk of total crop failure** · more efficient production per worker.

**Risks:** pests and fires strike several foods at once · land disputes involve several families · lack of seed compromises the whole cycle.

### Rotational Agriculture

Fields lose fertility after continuous use. The community can temporarily abandon the field, open a new controlled area, allow the vegetation to recover and reuse the area later.

Excessive deforestation reduces fertility, game and herb availability.

### Observation of the Cycles

The settlement informally records rainy periods, droughts, planting, harvest, hunting, fishing, the position of the Sun and the phases of the Moon.

Correct predictions improve agriculture, storage, morale and crisis preparation. Incorrect predictions cause seed loss and famine.

### Stone Age Missions

1. **Found the community** — build dwellings, secure water, create a field, fishing spot or hunting ground.
2. **Raise the Gathering Point** — gather five houses close together, build the site and **choose the Descent**.
3. **Organize the intercrop** — prepare the fields, distribute seed, secure cooperation between families.
4. **Feed the families** — produce food, diversify, build a granary, avoid famine.
5. **Know the territory** — explore the forest, map the river, locate useful plants, identify a safe area.
6. **Gather the lineages** — secure group support, resolve a rivalry, organize a community celebration.
7. **Master volcanic glass** — find an obsidian or flint deposit, build a workshop, produce tools and weapons.
8. **Protect the community** — build a palisade, create watchposts, organize defenders.
9. **Discover copper** — find ore, gather material, produce the first metal object.

### Unavailable Systems

Temple · patron deity · priesthood · Faith · ceremonial center · writing · codices · formal calendar · organized market · clan seat · clan missions · formal calpulli · taxation · urban government · standing army · districts · subordinate cities.

### Advancing to the Copper Age

- Sustain a stable population.
- Secure food and water.
- Organize agricultural production.
- Build a granary.
- Hold recognized leadership.
- Keep the lineages stable.
- Build a volcanic-glass workshop.
- Discover copper and produce a metal tool.
- Establish a permanent barter area.
- **Gathering Point built and Descent chosen.**

## What Happens in the Copper Age

The only Descent with three cultures — **nine deities available** at the first choice.

| Choice | Resulting culture | Copper deities |
|---|---|---|
| Tlaloc, Mixcoatl or Tezcatlipoca | Aztecs | [[Pantheon — Aztecs]] |
| Chaac, Xbalanqué or Kinich Ahau | Maya | [[Pantheon — Maya]] |
| Cairé/Rairé, Caipora/Curupira or Guaraci | Tupi-Guarani | [[Pantheon — Tupi-Guarani]] |

Systems that activate with Aztec culture: Tonalli · Tlatelolco Market · traveling merchants.
Systems that activate with Maya culture: K'uhul · Ceremonial Reservoirs · calendar.
Systems that activate with Tupi-Guarani culture: Teko · Balance of the Territory · Living Tekoá · Communal Sharing.
