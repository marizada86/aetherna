# Culture — Maya

**Category:** [[08 - Cultures and Pantheons]] · **Descent:** Americans · **Pantheon:** [[Pantheon — Maya]]

Seven pillars: agriculture adapted to the environment · water management · lineages and dynasties · ceremonial religion · astronomy and calendars · writing and monuments · **rivalry between city-states**.

| Era | Maya stage | Population | Main advance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stone | American Settlement | 20 | Descent chosen at the Gathering Point · no religion |
| Copper | Ceremonial Settlement | 50 | Temple, market, reservoirs and patron god |
| Bronze | City-State | 100 | Clan seats, writing, calendar, army and dynastic government |
| Iron | Dynastic Capital | 150 | Districts, network of cities, monuments, diplomacy and regional expansion |

## Identity

**Strengths:** astronomical knowledge · calendar planning · adaptable agriculture · reservoir management · ceremonial construction · writing and records · monumental prestige · diplomacy between cities · long-distance trade · religious specialization · **capacity to predict agricultural cycles**.

**Weaknesses:** rivalry between city-states · dependence on rains and reservoirs · high cost of temples and monuments · dynastic disputes · politically influential priesthood · **difficulty of regional centralization** · vulnerability to prolonged droughts · fragmentation among noble clans · excess of ceremonial obligations.

**Play style.** Cities planned around ceremonial centers, sustained by reservoirs, organized by lineages, dependent on calendar knowledge and specialized in Prestige, religion and diplomacy.

## Core cultural system — K'uhul

A value used in the system as **sacred legitimacy and ritual authority** of the city and its dynasty.

**Rises by:** observing ceremonies · maintaining temples · recording events · **erecting stelae** · preventing famine · controlling reservoirs · winning wars · making dynastic alliances · maintaining the calendar · protecting sacred sites · honoring the ancestors.

**Falls with:** drought · famine · military defeat · **destruction of stelae** · temple desecration · **serious calendar error** · succession crisis · rebellion of a subordinate city · abandonment of ceremonies · loss of reservoirs · disputes between lineages.

| High K'uhul | Low K'uhul |
|---|---|
| Greater loyalty · better religious efficiency · more Prestige · better diplomacy · lower rebellion risk · greater scribe productivity · greater lineage support · more efficient ceremonial buildings | Discontent · revolts · religious conflicts · loss of morale · falling production · dynastic disputes · less loyal subordinate cities · reduced regional Prestige |

## Stone Age — defined by Descent

This culture's Stone Age **does not exist separately**. It belongs to the Americans Descent and is identical for all its cultures.

See **[[Descent — Americans]]** for the full package: founding via the Gathering Point, social organization, government by in-person voting, buildings, units and cultural trait.

Maya identity begins only in the **Copper Age**, with the choice of the first deity. There is no religion in the Stone Age.

## Copper Age — Ceremonial Settlement (50)

The urban center forms around temple, ceremonial square, market, **reservoir** and the ruler's house.

**Available patron gods:** Chaac · Xbalanqué · Kinich Ahau.

**Government:** **ceremonial chief** assisted by priests and elders. Institutions: ruler's house, local council, market administrator, **reservoir supervisor**, **guardian of the calendar**, guard captain, informal family representatives. Authority depends on agricultural production, water control, religious support, organization of ceremonies, security and **K'uhul**.

**Economy:** copper, maize, beans, squash, chili peppers, cacao on a small scale, cotton, obsidian, flint, salt, timber, clay, feathers, herbs, honey. Production: copper tools, decorated pottery, textiles, baskets, dyes, weapons, ritual objects, feather ornaments, processed foods, **cacao drinks**, herbal remedies.

**New buildings:** adobe house, residential courtyard · expanded milpa, orchard, food processing house · copper mine and smelter, obsidian workshop, feather workshop, dye house · **reservoir**, canal, **rudimentary cistern**, **elevated walkway**, drainage area · public granary, **calendar house** · temple, ritual platform, **rudimentary observatory** · local market, barter square · wood and earth wall, patrol post.

**Military:** small standing guard — spearman with shield, archer, slinger, hunter, **obsidian blade warrior**, temple guard, scout, river patroller.

### Cultural trait — Ceremonial Reservoirs

Reservoirs near temples serve a **practical and religious** function.

- **Benefits:** water storage · protection against drought · improved agriculture · **more K'uhul during ceremonies**.
- **Risks:** poorly maintained reservoirs can contaminate the water, spread disease, **reduce K'uhul**, generate conflicts between families and compromise harvests.

### Early Calendar

Records seasons, rains, agricultural cycles, lunar phases, solar movement, festivals and **dynastic dates**. Correct predictions grant better agriculture, more efficient ceremonies, more Prestige and lower crisis risk.

**Missions:** raise the first temple · **build the reservoir** · organize the market · organize the crafts · form the guard · **observe the sky** · mediate the clans · open a trade route · **hold a great ceremony** · discover bronze.

**Advancing:** temple · patron god · market · **reservoir** · sustained priests · professions · standing guard · **early calendar recorded** · bronze · 50 citizens · controlled conflicts · clans ready for seats · public ceremony · stable K'uhul.

## Bronze Age — Maya City-State (100)

A **ruling dynasty**, clan seats and missions, formal council, **hieroglyphic writing**, organized calendars, **stelae**, standing army, great temples, regional trade, an **institutional ball game** and local tribute administration emerge.

**New gods:** Kukulcán · Ixchel · Huracán. Those of Copper **expire** on turning the era — the choice among the three of Bronze is mandatory. Switching to another culture of the same Descent triggers the [[Deities and Passives|Wrath of the Abandoned God]].

### Clans

Maya types: agricultural · mercantile · warrior · priestly · scribal · **astronomer** · artisanal · builder · **obsidian** · river · aristocratic · integrated foreign. See [[Clan System]].

### Government — the ajaw

The city is governed by an **ajaw**, tied to a dynasty. Institutions: palace, council of lineages, tribunal, treasury, archive, house of scribes, messengers' house, **reservoir administration**, market administration, ceremonial center.

| Form | Benefits | Risks |
|---|---|---|
| **Centralized dynasty** | More efficient great works · coordinated army · greater dynastic Prestige · fast decisions | Succession crisis · lineage revolts · **severe loss of legitimacy after defeat** |
| **Council of lineages** | Greater clan loyalty · better internal negotiation · funding of construction | Slow decisions · rivalries · political deadlock |
| **Priestly government** | High K'uhul · **more precise calendars** · more efficient ceremonies · better response to omens | High ritual cost · conflict with warriors · overly powerful priesthood |
| **Mercantile government** | More trade · greater revenue · better access to rare resources · expanded diplomacy | Corruption · monopolies · inequality · external dependence |

### Writing and Calendars

Allow recording dynasties, wars, alliances, tribute, harvests, omens, ceremonies, routes, constructions and important dates.

**Benefits:** better administration · greater Prestige · more K'uhul · better diplomacy · **lower risk of disputes over past events**.

### Stelae

They record public events: coronation · victory · founding · alliance · ceremony · completion of a temple · **astronomical event**.

**Each stela grants:** Prestige · legitimacy · historical memory · dynastic bonus.

**The destruction of a stela causes:** loss of K'uhul · **humiliation** · rivalry · a possible restoration mission.

**Buildings:** ajaw's palace, council of lineages, tribunal, treasury, archive, house of scribes, messengers' house, great market · clan seat, **lineage courtyard**, school, training ground · bronze smelter, obsidian workshop, **jade workshop**, dye house, great granary, **codex workshop**, sculptors' workshop · **great reservoir**, canal network, **early sacbé**, cistern, urban drainage, elevated roads · great market, mercantile district, collection post · stone and earth wall, **reservoir fortress** · **house of codices**, school of scribes, **observatory**, **ball court**, **stela**, school of healers, priest school.

**Military:** spearman, archer, slinger, shield warrior, obsidian blade warrior, temple guard, palace guard, scout, military messenger, veteran warrior, rudimentary siege engineer.

### Signature formation — Line of the Stelae

Units defending **monuments, temples or the palace** gain morale, defense, resistance to fear, greater cohesion and **a Prestige bonus for victory**.

Loses efficiency when the sacred area is destroyed, the commander is defeated or units are forced to abandon the site.

### Ball Game

May function as festival, **diplomatic competition**, ritual, Prestige event or **a way to settle rivalries**.

Results affect morale, diplomacy, K'uhul, clan prestige and relations between cities.

**Missions:** found the city-state · recognize the clans · create the house of scribes · **organize the calendar** · **build a dynastic stela** · expand the reservoirs · form a standing army · **raise a temple pyramid** · **open a regional route (sacbé)** · master iron.

**Advancing:** 100 citizens · clan seats · functioning dynastic government · clan missions · **organized writing and calendar** · **stela built** · great reservoir · walls · standing army · trade route · great temple · iron · avoid internal war · stable K'uhul · **subordinate or ally with another urban center**.

## Iron Age — Regional Dynastic Capital (150)

The city-state becomes a regional capital over villages, milpas, reservoirs, mines, markets, forts, **ceremonial centers**, allied cities, tributary cities and conquered territories.

**New gods:** Itzamná · Ah Puch · Camazotz. Those of Bronze **expire** on turning the era — the choice among the three of Iron is mandatory. Switching to another culture of the same Descent triggers the [[Deities and Passives|Wrath of the Abandoned God]].

### Forms of Government

| Form | Benefits | Risks |
|---|---|---|
| **Centralized sacred dynasty** | High K'uhul · efficient great works · coordinated army · better control of subordinate cities | Succession crises · rebellions · severe loss of legitimacy after defeat · **dependence on the ruling family** |
| **Council of houses** | Greater elite loyalty · better regional administration · more private funding | Rivalry · slow decisions · political fragmentation |
| **Priestly government** | Precise calendars · strong ceremonies · high K'uhul · **better prediction of environmental crises** | High ritual cost · conflict with the military · merchant resistance |
| **Military government** | Strong army · fast campaigns · greater tribute control · better defense | Coups · high military cost · rebellions after defeats · **less cultural development** |
| **League of cities** | More trade · more troops · lower administrative cost · expanded diplomacy | Unstable loyalty · **cities may abandon the league** · disputes over leadership |

**Economy:** regional taxation, tribute warehouses, market inspection, trade routes, long-distance merchants, tributary cities, monopolies, public food distribution, **measurement standards**, economic records.

**Products:** maize, cacao, cotton, salt, obsidian, **jade**, rare feathers, luxury pottery, textiles, **codices**, sculptures, weapons, iron tools, imported goods, religious objects.

**Buildings:** fortified palace, great council, central tribunal, **dynastic archive**, house of codices, messengers' house, embassy, tribute administration, **water administration** · expanded seat, lineage palace, **genealogical archive**, own school · iron mine, advanced irrigation, textile complex, jade workshop, codex workshop, sculptors' workshop · **great reservoirs**, cistern network, urban drainage, **regional sacbés**, simple aqueduct where applicable · great regional market, customs, tribute warehouse, river or coastal ports · fortified wall, citadel, military academy, forts along the routes · public cisterns, house of healing, **temple hospital**, bath or sweat houses · **great house of codices**, higher school of scribes, **monumental observatory**, **school of astronomers**, school of medicine, **great ball court**, botanical garden.

**Military:** iron spearman, veteran archer, veteran slinger, shield warrior, **obsidian warrior**, royal guard, temple guard, scout, military messenger, veteran warrior, engineer, siege unit, general, urban guard.

**Possible special units:** **Guard of the ajaw** · **Warrior of the night** · **Hunter of Xbalanqué** · Battle priest · **Cave scout** · **Protector of the reservoirs**.

### Cultural trait — Network of City-States

The capital may integrate subordinate centers, each specialized in agriculture, trade, religion, **writing**, war, craftsmanship, mining or **astronomy**.

**High loyalty:** more tribute · more troops · **more shared knowledge** · more routes opened · greater K'uhul for the capital.
**Dissatisfied cities may:** withhold tribute · **destroy the ruler's stelae** · back pretenders · refuse troops · form alliances · rebel.

### Dynastic Monument System

Stelae, altars, pyramids, palaces, observatories, ball courts, sacbés and dynastic tombs.

**They grant:** Prestige · K'uhul · legitimacy · **pilgrimage** · historical memory · influence over subordinate cities.

**Incomplete projects cause:** loss of Prestige · worker dissatisfaction · resource waste · **questioning of the dynasty**.

### Dynastic Diplomacy

Marriages · gift exchange · **joint stelae** · ceremonial games · treaties · vassalage · adoption of heirs · recognition of dynasties.

Diplomatic marriages can end wars, create alliances, reinforce legitimacy and **generate succession claims**.

**Missions:** conduct the great census · reform the council · expand the reservoirs · **standardize the calendars** · build a monumental pyramid · **create a network of sacbés** · form a regional army · administer the districts · organize a regional festival · **resolve a dynastic crisis** · suppress or negotiate a rebellion · establish regional supremacy.

**Era risks:** prolonged drought · **reservoir collapse** · famine · epidemic · war between cities · tribute rebellion · succession crisis · coup · conflict between priests and military · **destruction of stelae** · **loss of codices** · route blockade · administrative corruption · political fragmentation · loss of K'uhul · abandoned works · invasion · worker revolt.

## Progression Summary

| Element | Stone | Copper | Bronze | Iron |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Urban form | American Settlement | Ceremonial settlement | City-state | Dynastic capital |
| Government | Chief and elders | Ceremonial chief | Ajaw and council | Regional dynasty or league of cities |
| Religion | **None** | Temple patron | Urban ceremonial system | Regional religious system |
| Agriculture | Rudimentary milpa | Organized agriculture | Irrigation and reservoirs | Intensive regional production |
| Military | Communal defense | Local guard | Standing army | Regional army |
| Culture | Oral tradition | Early calendar | Writing, stelae and ball game | Codices, observatories and monuments |
| Expansion | Survival | Regional contact | Influence of the city-state | Subordinate cities and dynastic supremacy |

**Identity of each era:** Stone, Americans Descent (see [[Descent — Americans]]) · Copper, temple and reservoir · Bronze, dynasty and writing · Iron, monuments and network of cities.
