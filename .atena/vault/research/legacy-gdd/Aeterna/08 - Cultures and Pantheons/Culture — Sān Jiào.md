# Culture — Sān Jiào

**Category:** [[08 - Cultures and Pantheons]] · **Descent:** Easterners · **Pantheon:** [[Pantheon — Sān Jiào]]

> **Cultural adaptation note (from the source).** Sān Jiào means "Three Teachings" and represents the coexistence of distinct religious, philosophical and social traditions. In this playable culture the term is used as an **internal syncretic structure of the setting**, gathering ancestor worship, household religion, bureaucratic administration, agriculture, cosmology, social harmony, celestial deities, territorial spirits, martial traditions, family order and government legitimized by Heaven.
>
> **The game must not present Sān Jiào as a uniform historical pantheon.** The culture is a fictional synthesis built for the system.

Seven pillars: family and ancestry · organized agriculture · social harmony · household and state worship · bureaucracy · technical knowledge · Mandate of Heaven.

| Era | Cultural stage | Population | Main advance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stone | Eastern Settlement | 20 | Descent chosen at the Gathering Point · no religion |
| Copper | Settlement of Altars | 50 | Temple, market, professions, calendar and patron |
| Bronze | Walled City of Crafts | 100 | Clan seats, bureaucracy, schools, army and own missions |
| Iron | Capital Under Heaven | 150 (capital) | Districts, examinations, provinces, great works and Mandate of Heaven |

## Identity

**Strengths:** administration · intensive agriculture · family organization · artisanal production · education · medicine · infrastructure · river control · city defense · social stability · bureaucracy · public works · **capacity to absorb different groups**.

**Weaknesses:** expensive bureaucracy · corruption · slow administrative decisions · conflicts between clans and officials · dependence on stable harvests · vulnerability to floods · pressure to maintain harmony and legitimacy · disputes between cults · peasant revolts · **risk of losing the Mandate of Heaven** · excessive centralization · nepotism.

**Play style.** Planned, walled cities organized into districts, sustained by intensive agriculture, administered by officials and connected by roads, canals and messengers. Power depends on Harmony, legitimacy, agricultural production, administrative efficiency, education, observance of rites, respect for ancestors, control of the waters and confidence in the government.

## Core cultural system — Harmony

The balance between family, government, production, religion, nature, social hierarchy, justice and communal obligations.

**Rises by:** keeping food available · resolving disputes · honoring ancestors · observing rites · controlling floods · keeping competent officials · avoiding corruption · promoting education · building infrastructure · protecting families · ensuring justice · integrating different clans.

**Falls with:** famine · corruption · nepotism · flood · drought · revolt · broken rites · abandonment of the ancestors · excessive taxation · civil war · incompetent officials · disease · extreme inequality.

| High Harmony | Low Harmony |
|---|---|
| Greater productivity · more loyalty · less crime · better administrative efficiency · more Prestige · greater religious efficiency · better diplomacy · lower risk of revolt | Corruption · crime · strikes · rebellions · lost production · disobedience · family conflicts · factions · reduced legitimacy · **population exodus** |

## Core political system — Mandate of Heaven

The ruler's cosmic legitimacy. Fully unlocked in the Iron Age, though its foundations appear earlier.

**Rises when the ruler:** maintains order · feeds the population · controls disasters · governs justly · wins defensive wars · observes the rites · appoints competent officials · avoids corruption · protects families · builds public works.

**Falls with:** prolonged famine · badly managed disasters · military defeats · widespread corruption · revolts · succession crises · ritual abandonment · a cruel or incompetent ruler · infrastructure collapse.

**A low Mandate justifies:** rebellions · pretenders · provincial secession · coups · **dynastic change**.

## Stone Age — defined by Descent

This culture's Stone Age **does not exist separately**. It belongs to the Easterners Descent and is identical for all its cultures.

See **[[Descent — Easterners]]** for the full package: founding via the Gathering Point, social organization, government by in-person voting, buildings, units and cultural trait.

Sān Jiào identity begins only in the **Copper Age**, with the choice of the first deity. There is no religion in the Stone Age.

## Copper Age — Settlement of Altars (50)

The urban center forms around temple, ancestral altar, market, granary, ruler's house and community square.

**Available patrons:** Shennong · Chang'e and Houyi · Xihe/Taiyang Shen.

**Government:** **chief magistrate** supported by families and priests. Institutions: ruler's house, local council, granary administrator, field supervisor, market administrator, **guardian of the calendar**, guard captain, council of elders. Authority depends on food production, justice, family support, observance of rites, security and **Harmony**.

**Economy:** copper, grain, rice, millet, fish, rudimentary silk where applicable, bamboo, timber, clay, tea or herbs, leather, **salt**, fruit, medicinal plants. Production: copper tools, pottery, textiles, baskets, **rudimentary paper or writing supports**, river boats, weapons, herbal remedies, ritual objects, processed foods, oils, fermented drinks.

**New buildings:** wooden house, adobe house · **paddy field**, millet field, **medicinal garden**, orchard, manual mill · copper mine and smelter, bamboo workshop, **herb house**, river shipyard · irrigation canal, reservoir, rudimentary dike, wooden bridge, simple drainage · **calendar house**, public granary, registry post · temple, ancestral altar, **household altar**, **rudimentary observatory** · local market, caravan station, commercial dock · wood and earth wall, river post.

**Military:** small standing guard — spearman with shield, archer, hunter, temple guard, river patroller, scout, copper-sword warrior, **messenger**.

### Cultural trait — Household Altars

Each family compound can build an ancestral altar.

- **Benefits:** more Harmony · higher morale · lower impact of deaths · better family loyalty · **small inherited professional bonus**.
- **Risks:** families may develop **rival cults** · neglect breeds dissatisfaction · altars of powerful families increase their influence.

### Agricultural Calendar

Records rains, seasons, lunar phases, planting periods, harvests, festivals and solar events. Correct predictions raise agriculture, trade, Harmony and ritual efficiency.

**Missions:** raise the first temple · organize the fields · build a public granary · organize the market · **create a house of medicine** · form the guard · **organize the calendar** · mediate the families · open a river or land route · discover bronze.

**Advancing:** temple · patron · organized agriculture · granary · market · professions · standing guard · **calendar system** · bronze · 50 citizens · controlled conflicts · clans ready for seats · **stable Harmony**.

## Bronze Age — Walled City of Crafts (100)

A walled city organized by districts, crafts and permanent institutions. Clan seats and missions, formal council, bureaucracy, records, schools, standing army, great temples, regional trade, tribute, administrative laws and **professional guilds** emerge.

**New patrons:** Chiyou · Jiutian Xuannü · Zao Jun. Those of Copper **expire** on turning the era — the choice among the three of Bronze is mandatory. Switching to another culture of the same Descent triggers the [[Deities and Passives|Wrath of the Abandoned God]].

### Clans

Types: agricultural · mercantile · military · priestly · artisanal · **medical** · scribal · builder · river · aristocratic · **messenger** · integrated foreign. See [[Clan System]].

Unlocks include a **family school**.

### Craft Guilds

Professions may form recognized guilds: smiths · potters · weavers · carpenters · healers · merchants · builders · boatmen · scribes · messengers.

They may train apprentices, set standards, fund works, control prices, request privileges and **come into conflict with clans**.

### Forms of Government

| Form | Benefits | Risks |
|---|---|---|
| **Bureaucratic government** | Better taxation · less waste · efficient infrastructure · better population control | Corruption · slow decisions · expensive administration · excess records |
| **Council of clans** | Greater loyalty · more funding · lower risk of authoritarianism | Political deadlock · rivalry · exclusion of smaller families |
| **Military government** | Strong army · fast response · better defense | Coups · high military cost · **lower Harmony** · dependence on victories |
| **Ritual government** | High Harmony · efficient rites · better response to omens · less spiritual instability | High religious cost · conflicts between cults · resistance from merchants and military |
| **Mercantile government** | More trade · greater revenue · expanded routes · access to rare resources | Monopolies · corruption · inequality · external dependence |

### Official System

Officials serve as administrators, scribes, judges, collectors, inspectors, engineers, supervisors and diplomats.

The more complex the city, the more officials are required, the higher the administrative cost and the more their competence matters.

**Incompetent or corrupt officials cause:** resource loss · delays · revolts · falsification · falling Harmony.

### Early Merit System

Offices may be granted by kinship, prestige, recommendation, knowledge or **administrative examination**.

| Favoring merit | Favoring kinship |
|---|---|
| More efficiency · less corruption · better infrastructure | More clan loyalty · less family opposition · **greater risk of incompetence** |

**Buildings:** administrative palace, council, tribunal, treasury, public archive, house of scribes, **records center**, messengers' house · clan seat, **school**, lineage house, private dock · bronze smelter, **silk workshop**, bamboo workshop, great granary, **writing workshop**, house of medicine · main canal, dikes, reservoirs, paved roads, **messenger posts** · great market, **silk market**, caravan house, river port · rammed-earth or stone wall, river fort · school of scribes, **early classical school**, map workshop, observatory, military school, **ancestral archive**.

**Military:** spearman, archer, early crossbowman (where compatible with the setting's technology), shield warrior, palace guard, temple guard, scout, river patroller, light cavalry, siege engineer, officer.

### Signature formation — Wall of Spears

Units in line gain frontal defense, resistance to charges, greater discipline and **better coordination with archers**.

Loses efficiency when flanked, surrounded, separated from officers or moved over uneven terrain.

### Cultural trait — City of a Hundred Crafts

Districts may specialize in pottery, weaving, metallurgy, medicine, trade, writing, construction, navigation or food production.

**Specialized districts gain:** more productivity · apprentices · Prestige · trade.
**They may also generate:** monopolies · strikes · rivalry · **pollution** · fires.

**Missions:** found the urban administration · recognize the clans · create a formal council · organize the crafts · control the waters · form a standing army · create a school of scribes · raise a great temple · open a regional route · master iron.

**Advancing:** 100 citizens · clan seats · functioning council · clan missions · **permanent bureaucracy** · maintained schools · controlled canals · walls · army · trade route · great temple · iron · avoid internal war · high Harmony · **administer at least one subordinate settlement**.

## Iron Age — Capital Under Heaven (150 in the capital)

> Subordinate cities, villages and provinces have their own populations and are administered separately.

**New patrons:** Jade Emperor (Yuhuang Dadi) · Dragon Kings (Longwang) · Yanluo Wang. Those of Bronze **expire** on turning the era — the choice among the three of Iron is mandatory. Switching to another culture of the same Descent triggers the [[Deities and Passives|Wrath of the Abandoned God]].

### Forms of Government

| Form | Benefits | Risks |
|---|---|---|
| **Bureaucratic monarchy** | Better provincial control · efficient taxation · advanced infrastructure · less waste | Corruption · expensive administration · slow decisions · powerful officials |
| **Aristocratic government** | Greater elite support · better local administration · more private funding | Nepotism · fragmentation · dominant clans · succession crises |
| **Military government** | Strong army · fast expansion · better defense | Coups · high military cost · lower Harmony · rivalry between commanders |
| **Ritual government** | High Mandate · better response to omens · efficient rites · greater spiritual stability | High ritual cost · administrative superstition · conflict with bureaucrats · religious disputes |
| **Meritocratic government** | Competent officials · less corruption · greater social mobility · better administration | High educational cost · aristocratic resistance · competition between families · **exclusion of citizens without access to schools** |

### Examination System

Examinations select officials based on writing, administration, laws, mathematics, history, rites, strategy and agricultural knowledge.

**Benefits:** better officials · less corruption · greater efficiency · more social mobility · better provincial control.

**Risks:** wealthy families dominate the schools · fraud · purchased answers · aristocratic resistance · **formation of academic factions**.

### Province System

Each province has production, population, loyalty, a governor, tribute, a garrison, cults, clans and infrastructure.

The player may appoint governors, replace officials, adjust tribute, build canals, fund schools, send troops, integrate clans and carry out inspections.

**Dissatisfied provinces may:** withhold tribute · falsify records · back pretenders · refuse soldiers · rebel · **declare the Mandate lost**.

### Public Works System

Canals, dikes, roads, bridges, walls, granaries, aqueducts, ports, temples and academies grant Harmony, legitimacy, production, mobility, security and Prestige.

**Abandoned works cause:** corruption · waste · revolts · **loss of the Mandate** · environmental damage.

**Buildings:** fortified palace, audience hall, higher council, imperial archive, mint, **ministry of waters**, **ministry of tribute**, **ministry of rites**, governors' hall · expanded seat, clan palace, **genealogical archive**, own school · iron mine, **silk complex**, **paper workshop**, advanced irrigation, water mill, advanced shipyard · great market, river and military ports, credit house, state warehouse · fortified wall, citadel, **signal towers**, river forts · aqueduct, cisterns, public baths, firefighting corps · academy, library, **school of officials**, school of medicine, observatory, cartography workshop, **music hall**, **medicinal garden**, ancestral archive.

**Military:** iron spearman, veteran archer, crossbowman (per the setting's technology), shield warrior, palace guard, temple guard, cavalry and elite cavalry, scout, war sailor, engineer, siege unit, general, urban guard.

**Possible special units:** Celestial guard · **Strategist of Jiutian Xuannü** · Warrior of Chiyou · Engineer of the Dragon Kings · **Magistrate of Yanluo** · Imperial messenger.

### Signature formation — Lines of the Empire

Units under officers gain discipline, frontal defense, morale, resistance to disorganization, better logistics and greater command efficiency.

Loses efficiency when officers are defeated, **messengers are intercepted**, supplies run out or the line is surrounded.

### Cultural trait — Capital of Ten Thousand Records

Each sector produces records on population, harvests, tribute, army, trade, families, clans, officials, crime, works and **omens**.

Correct records raise efficiency, revenue, planning, provincial control and Harmony.

**An excess of records without enough officials** causes delays, confusion, corruption and **administrative paralysis**.

**Missions:** conduct the census · **create the examination system** · reform the provinces · control the great waters · **build a monumental wall** · modernize agriculture · found an academy · administer the districts · standardize currency and measures · create a diplomatic network · resolve a clan crisis · **resolve a Mandate crisis** · resolve a succession crisis · establish regional supremacy.

**Era risks:** flood · drought · famine · epidemic · corruption · nepotism · **examination fraud** · peasant revolt · military coup · provincial rebellion · succession crisis · clan conflict · **loss of the Mandate of Heaven** · trade blockade · canal collapse · urban fire · invasion · abandoned works · **paralyzed bureaucracy** · civil war.
