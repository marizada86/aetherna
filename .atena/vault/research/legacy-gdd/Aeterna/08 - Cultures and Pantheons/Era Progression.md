# Era Progression

**Category:** [[08 - Cultures and Pantheons]]

Generic rules for city evolution. The **Stone Age** is defined by Descent and is identical for all of its cultures; from **Copper** onward each culture clothes this skeleton with its own names, buildings, units and governments — see [[Culture System]].

Evolution happens along three axes: **population capacity**, **building complexity** and **social, political, economic and religious organization**.

## Founding and Descent Choice

```
5 houses built close together in a habitable zone
  → unlocks construction of the Gathering Point
    → players choose the Descent
      → the city acquires that Descent's prehistoric package
```

The **Gathering Point** is the first political structure in the game. There, the city's next steps are decided by **in-person voting** — the orality of the Stone Age. No writing, no board, no remote voting: whoever is not physically present does not vote.

It is the direct ancestor of [[The Town Hall and the Council]], which only emerges when the culture activates in Copper.

**Stone Age notes by Descent:** [[Descent — Westerners]] · [[Descent — Easterners]] · [[Descent — Africans]] · [[Descent — Americans]]

## The Four Eras

| Era | Urban stage | Main advance | Max population |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stone | Village | Survival, families and the first clans | 20 citizens |
| Copper | Settlement | Metallurgy, organized religion and specialization | 50 citizens |
| Bronze | City | Clan seats, clan missions, bureaucracy and regional trade | 100 citizens |
| Iron | Regional capital | Districts, public infrastructure and territorial expansion | 150 citizens |

The limits are each era's default. Special buildings, laws, technologies, religious effects or government decisions can raise them.

## Stone Age — Village

**Defined by Descent, not by culture.** The two or three cultures of a single Descent have **identical** Stone Ages. A western settlement is indistinguishable between future Norse and future Greek until the first temple.

**Identity.** A village dependent on hunting, gathering, fishing, rudimentary animal husbandry and early agriculture. Society organized by families and informal clans based on kinship, tradition and cooperation.

**Building categories:** Housing · Food · Production · Infrastructure · Administration · Defense.

### There Is No Religion

The Stone Age **has no religion in any Descent**: no temple, no priesthood, no patron deity, no Faith generation, no religious buildings.

Burials exist as a **social custom**, with no mechanical effect. The city's spiritual life begins only in the Copper Age, with the first temple and the first deity.

**City missions:** found the settlement · **raise the Gathering Point and choose the Descent** · feed the community · master stone · gather the clans · protect the village · establish leadership · begin cultivation · discover copper.

**Available systems:** families, informal clans, tribal leadership, elders, in-person voting at the Gathering Point, hunting and gathering, rudimentary agriculture, direct barter, communal defense, family rivalries, marriages between clans.

**Unavailable:** clan seat, clan missions, formal council of clans, **religion of any kind**, temple, priesthood, Faith, patron deity, taxation, formal government, standing army, organized market, writing.

**Advancing to Copper:** sustain a stable population · produce a food surplus · hold recognized leadership · keep clans relatively stable · have a stable water source · discover copper · produce copper tools or objects · **Gathering Point built and Descent chosen**.

## Copper Age — Settlement

**Identity.** Specialized professions emerge: farmers, miners, artisans, warriors, priests. **Religion is born here.** The city builds its first temple and chooses a deity — and that choice **defines the city's culture** within its Descent.

From this point on, all cultural traits take effect.

**New building categories:** Trade (local market, trading post, dock, mercantile warehouse, caravan station) and institutional Religion (temple, priests' house, shrine, ceremonial square).

**City missions:** choose a patron deity · organize the crafts · explore the mines · create a market · form a guard · organize the settlement · expand the cult · mediate clan interests · develop external trade · discover bronze.

**Religious mechanic.** Building the first temple allows choosing the patron deity among the three of that culture and that era. **The deity is era-locked** — on advancing to Bronze it expires and a new choice is mandatory. See [[Deities and Passives]].

Divine domains and the benefit/requirement pairing:

| Divine domain | Possible benefit | Possible requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Agriculture | Greater food production | Harvest offerings |
| War | Better guard performance | Training and campaigns |
| Trade | Better exchanges and caravans | Building markets |
| Knowledge | Faster development | Sustaining priests |
| Nature | Better resource yield | Limits on exploitation |
| Sea | Better fishing and navigation | Coastal buildings |
| Death | Lower impact of funerals and epidemics | Funerary rites |
| Forge | Better tool production | Metal offerings |

Concrete passives for each deity are in [[Deities and Passives]].

**Unavailable:** clan seat, clan missions, formal council of clans, clan territories, clan armies, institutionalized nobility, fully organized city-state.

**Advancing to Bronze:** build a temple · choose a patron deity · establish a market · sustain specialized workers · maintain a standing guard · produce bronze · control conflicts between clans · hold resources for the first clan seats.

## Bronze Age — City

**Identity.** Permanent administration, regional trade, specialized production and structured military forces. **Clans become formal institutions** — they can build seats and offer their own missions. See [[Clan System]].

**New building categories:** Clan buildings (seat, meeting hall, warehouse, workshop, shrine, courtyard) and Culture (school of scribes, artistic workshop, ceremonial square, public monument, ritual arena, festival hall).

The clan seat is the central building; the others may require upgrades or specializations of it.

**City missions:** build the first clan seats · form the council of clans · balance power · found the urban administration · record the laws · control the waters · open a trade route · raise a great temple · form a standing army · master iron.

**New systems:** clan seats, clan missions, recognized chieftains, council of clans, political influence, alliances, rivalries, political marriages, social classes, formal taxation, writing, laws, diplomacy, regional trade, standing army, urban prestige, rebellions, succession disputes.

**Advancing to Iron:** hold formally recognized clans · build seats for the main clans · complete clan missions · maintain a functioning council · avoid internal war · sustain central administration · control a trade route · hold stone walls · maintain a standing army · master iron metallurgy.

## Iron Age — Regional Capital

**Identity.** Iron metallurgy expands agriculture, construction, production and military capacity. Clans compete for influence with the central government, army, priesthood and merchants. **The challenge stops being founding the city and becomes administering its complexity** and preventing any one clan from growing too powerful.

**New building categories:** Urban services (aqueduct, cistern, drainage network, baths, house of healing, firefighting, urban guard, waste collection, food distribution) and Culture and knowledge (library, academy, theater, arena, forum, monuments, engineering workshop, school of administration).

**City missions:** conduct the first census · reform the council of clans · modernize agriculture · build regional infrastructure · found a citadel · administer the districts · standardize currency · create a diplomatic network · resolve a crisis between clans · establish regional supremacy.

**New systems:** urban districts, citizenship, census, currency, public health, sanitation, corruption, crime, migration, political factions, guilds, noble houses, dynasties, competing cults, subordinate cities, provinces, military logistics, wars of expansion, succession crises, dominant clans, clan rebellions, dynastic succession.

## Direct Parallel Between the Eras

| Element | Stone | Copper | Bronze | Iron |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Urban stage | Village | Settlement | City | Regional capital |
| Max population | 20 | 50 | 100 | 150 |
| Social organization | Families and informal clans | Families, professions and clans | Institutionalized clans | Clans, noble houses and factions |
| Clan seat | Unavailable | Unavailable | Available | Can be expanded |
| Clan missions | Unavailable | Unavailable | Available | Available and more complex |
| Political organization | Tribal chief | Local council | Urban administration and council of clans | Regional government |
| Food | Hunting, gathering and cultivation | Organized agriculture | Irrigation and surplus | Extensive agriculture |
| Metallurgy | Stone and bone | Copper | Bronze | Iron |
| Religion | **None** | Temple and patron deity | Institutional cult | Complex religious system |
| Identity | Descent | **Culture defined by the deity** | Culture | Culture |
| Defense | Communal defense | Local guard | Standing army | Regional army |
| Trade | Direct barter | Local market | Regional routes | Trade network and currency |
| Administration | Informal leadership | Local administration | Bureaucracy | Complex government |
| Clan influence | Social | Economic and religious | Political and institutional | Regional and dynastic |

## Recommended Mission Structure

Each era should have four main categories, plus a fifth from Bronze onward:

1. **Survival** — food, water, housing, health, security.
2. **Development** — buildings, production, technology, infrastructure.
3. **Society** — religion, government, families, clans, laws, culture, stability.
4. **Expansion** — exploration, trade, diplomacy, war, territorial control.
5. **Clan missions** — unlocked only in the Bronze Age. They cover economic interests, prestige, rivalries, religion, politics, buildings, trade, war, marriages and territorial control.
