# Culture — Egyptians

**Category:** [[08 - Cultures and Pantheons]] · **Descent:** Africans · **Pantheon:** [[Pantheon — Egyptians]]

Six pillars: river cycles · irrigated agriculture · religious authority · resource administration · funerary cult · monumental construction.

The central characteristic is the transformation of a riverside community dependent on floods into a centralized, bureaucratic and religiously legitimized capital.

| Era | Egyptian stage | Population | Main advance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stone | African Settlement | 20 | Descent chosen at the Gathering Point · no religion |
| Copper | Temple Settlement | 50 | Temple, organized irrigation, priesthood and patron god |
| Bronze | City of the Nome | 100 | Clan seats, bureaucracy, laws, army and great works |
| Iron | Capital of the Kingdom | 150 | Districts, regional administration, monuments and territorial expansion |

## Identity

**Strengths:** irrigated agriculture · food storage · centralized administration · monumental construction · institutional religion · water control · **papyrus production** · medicine · mining · long-term stability · river trade.

**Weaknesses:** dependence on the river · vulnerability to droughts and extreme floods · high cost of temples and monuments · **politically powerful priesthood** · costly bureaucracy · difficulty responding quickly to crises · disputes between central administration and clans · risk of provincial revolts · dependence on public stores.

**Play style.** Planned, irrigated, centralized, religiously organized cities sustained by great reserves. Power depends on control of the waters, agricultural surplus, religious legitimacy, administrative efficiency, funerary stability, monumental prestige and the ruler's authority.

## Core cultural system — Ma'at

A value exclusive to the culture, representing **order, balance, justice and continuity**.

**Rises by:** keeping food available · controlling the floods · observing rites · resolving disputes · keeping laws stable · **protecting the dead** · paying workers · avoiding corruption · preserving temples and canals.

**Falls with:** famine · corruption · **tomb desecration** · civil war · abandonment of rites · administrative failure · excessive taxation · revolts · destruction of canals · succession crises.

| High Ma'at | Low Ma'at |
|---|---|
| Greater productivity · better morale · more loyalty · greater administrative efficiency · lower crime risk · greater religious Prestige · better clan stability | Corruption · disobedience · theft · religious conflicts · disease · revolts · lost production · **questioning of the ruler's legitimacy** |

## Stone Age — defined by Descent

This culture's Stone Age **does not exist separately**. It belongs to the Africans Descent and is identical for all its cultures.

See **[[Descent — Africans]]** for the full package: founding via the Gathering Point, social organization, government by in-person voting, buildings, units and cultural trait.

Egyptian identity begins only in the **Copper Age**, with the choice of the first deity. There is no religion in the Stone Age.

## Copper Age — Temple Settlement (50)

Agriculture begins to use canals, reservoirs, rudimentary dikes, delimited fields and public granaries. The urban center forms around temple, public granary, ceremonial square and the ruler's house.

**Available patron gods:** Osiris · Isis · Sobek.

**Government:** **priest-ruler** or local chief legitimized by the temple. Institutions: ruler's house, local council, granary administrator, **water supervisor**, resource registrar, guard captain, priestly council. Authority depends on control of food, organization of the floods, priestly support, fair land distribution and **maintenance of Ma'at**.

**Economy:** copper, grain, **flax**, **papyrus**, fish, clay, stone, imported timber, medicinal herbs, leather, reeds, gold on a small scale. Production: copper tools, pottery, linen textiles, rudimentary papyrus, river boats, simple jewelry, religious figurines, dried fish, beer, bread.

**New buildings:** adobe house · irrigated field, bakery, brewery, medicinal garden · copper mine and smelter, linen weaving, **papyrus workshop**, jewelry workshop, river shipyard · irrigation canal, rudimentary dike, reservoir, dock, **water measurement marker** · public granary, water supervisor's house · temple, **funerary house**, ancestral chapel · local market, river trading post · wood and adobe wall, river post.

**Military:** small standing guard — spearman with shield, archer, slinger, temple guard, **river patroller**, armed sailor, scout, club warrior.

### Cultural trait — Temple Granaries

Temples can store and redistribute food.

- **Benefits:** lower harvest loss · better famine resistance · greater priestly loyalty · more efficient offerings · capacity to sustain public workers.
- **Risks (excessive temple control):** the priesthood gains influence · **the ruler loses authority** · agricultural clans become dependent · corruption and resource diversion.

### The River Flood Cycle

The year divides into four functional phases:

| Phase | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Flood** | Low areas inundated · fishing increases · agriculture halted · buildings near the river vulnerable |
| **Recession** | Fertile land becomes available · canals can be repaired · fields must be marked again |
| **Planting** | Farmers occupy the fields · seed is distributed · tools are essential |
| **Harvest** | Food is stored · the population builds reserves · informal tribute may be collected |

### Water Measurement System

Recording flood intensity allows adjusting planting, reducing damage, preparing stores, improving irrigation and planning tribute.

**Incorrect predictions cause:** famine · waste · revolts · loss of Ma'at · **religious discredit**.

**Missions:** raise the first temple · control the waters · organize the granary · organize the crafts · form the guard · **produce papyrus** · mediate the clans · open a river route · **refine the funerary rites** · discover bronze.

**Advancing:** temple · patron god · basic irrigation · public granary · sustained priests · specialized professions · standing guard · market and river route · bronze · 50 citizens · controlled conflicts · clans ready for seats.

## Bronze Age — City of the Nome (100)

The settlement becomes the center of a **nome**: a territorial division formed by a main city, rural villages, fields, canals, mines, temples and river ports.

Administrative writing, scribes, formal taxation, clan seats and missions, permanent government, laws, a structured army, great temples and public works emerge.

**New gods:** Thoth · Sekhmet · Anubis. Those of Copper **expire** on turning the era — the choice among the three of Bronze is mandatory. Switching to another culture of the same Descent triggers the [[Deities and Passives|Wrath of the Abandoned God]].

### Clans

Egyptian types: agricultural · **irrigators** · priestly · scribal · mercantile · military · **funerary** · artisanal · mining · naval · aristocratic · integrated foreign. See [[Clan System]].

### Government — the nomarch

The city is governed by a **nomarch**, responsible for the territory. Institutions: nomarch's palace, council of clans, house of scribes, tribunal, treasury, archive, collection post, water administration, agricultural administration.

| Administrative form | Benefits | Risks |
|---|---|---|
| **Priestly government** | Greater Faith · religious stability · productive temples · efficient rites | Priests accumulate power · higher cost of offerings · conflicts with scribes and military |
| **Bureaucratic government** | Better taxation · less waste · better resource control · efficient public buildings | Corruption · expensive administration · slow decisions · popular dissatisfaction |
| **Aristocratic government** | More private funding · greater elite loyalty · better specialized workers | Inequality · rivalries · smaller clans lose influence |
| **Military government** | Fast recruitment · better defense · more efficient campaigns | High military cost · politically powerful generals · risk of coup |

**Buildings:** nomarch's palace, house of scribes, public archive, tribunal, **water office** · clan seat, records house, naval clan dock · bronze smelter, main canal, great granary, papyrus workshop, **funerary object workshop**, sculptors' workshop · great temple, **necropolis**, **embalming house**, funerary temple, mortuary chapel · great market, river port, caravan house · stone or reinforced adobe wall, river fort · school of scribes, **house of life**, school of medicine, **astronomy workshop**.

**Military:** spearman, archer, axe warrior, temple guard, shield infantry, scout, war sailor, **early chariot**, nomarch's guard, rudimentary siege engineer.

### Signature formation — Line of the Nile

Units organized in a **continuous line** gain better frontal defense, greater accuracy for archers positioned behind, better resistance against charges and greater command efficiency.

Loses efficiency when flanked, separated by terrain, broken by heavy units or deprived of officers.

### Cultural trait — Administration of the Scribes

Scribes increase the efficiency of granaries, canals, tribute, buildings, trade, the army and temples. The more complex the city, the more scribes are required.

**A shortage of scribes causes:** resource loss · taxation errors · delays · corruption · **reduced Ma'at**.

### Funerary System

Deaths must be administered: preparation of the body · burial · offerings · registration · maintenance of the tomb.

**Proper funerals grant:** stability · Faith · Prestige · less morale loss.
**Neglected funerals cause:** disease · family revolts · **hauntings** · loss of Ma'at · priestly conflicts.

**Missions:** found the nome · recognize the clans · create the house of scribes · control the waters · record the tribute · form the army · raise a great temple · **build the necropolis** · open a regional route · master iron.

**Advancing:** 100 citizens · clan seats · functioning council · clan missions · scribal administration · controlled canals and agricultural production · walls · standing army · trade route · great temple · **organized necropolis** · iron · avoid internal war · stable Ma'at.

## Iron Age — Capital of the Kingdom (150)

The capital governs nomes, villages, mines, ports, forts, temples, subordinate cities, agricultural territories and caravan routes. **Administration becomes highly centralized.**

**New gods:** Ra/Amun-Ra · Horus · Set. Those of Bronze **expire** on turning the era — the choice among the three of Iron is mandatory. Switching to another culture of the same Descent triggers the [[Deities and Passives|Wrath of the Abandoned God]].

### Forms of Government

| Form | Benefits | Risks |
|---|---|---|
| **Centralized divine kingship** | High legitimacy · more efficient great works · better coordinated army · less provincial autonomy | High administrative cost · **severe crisis when the ruler loses legitimacy** · succession disputes |
| **Priestly kingship** | Greater Faith · religious stability · productive temples · lower impact of funerary crises | Overly powerful priesthood · conflicts with generals and scribes · expensive offerings |
| **Administration of the nomarchs** | More productive provinces · fast response to local crises · lower central cost | Rebellions · fragmentation · provincial dynasties · withheld tribute |
| **Military government** | Fast recruitment · better defense · efficient campaigns · border control | Coups · high military cost · lower civil satisfaction · rivalry between commanders |

**Economy:** mint, central taxation, customs, regional granaries, mercantile credit, river trade, caravan routes, provincial tribute, **state mining**, public food distribution.

**Products:** grain, fine linen, papyrus, **gold**, jewelry, statues, funerary objects, iron weapons, chariots, luxury pottery, **rudimentary glass**, imported incense and timber, administrative and religious texts.

**Buildings:** fortified palace, throne room, royal archive, mint, **ministry of waters**, **ministry of granaries**, **nomarchs' hall** · expanded seat, clan palace, lineage archive, private dock · iron mine, primitive blast furnace, advanced irrigation, water mill, **monument workshop**, **glass workshop** · great market, river and military ports, credit house, royal warehouse · fortified wall, citadel, **desert forts**, river towers · aqueduct, cistern, baths, **temple hospital**, public canal system · **great house of life**, library, school of medicine, observatory, cartography workshop, **sacred garden**.

**Selectable monumental constructions:** great solar temple · funerary complex · **obelisk** · ceremonial avenue · **colossus** · dynastic temple · royal tomb · monumental palace. They demand many resources, workers, scribes, priests, logistical administration and political stability.

**Military:** iron spearman, veteran archer, shield infantry, royal guard, temple guard, **chariot** and elite chariot, **desert scout**, war sailor, engineer, siege unit, general, urban guard. **River:** merchant boat, patrol boat, transport ship, warship, supply vessel, **ceremonial ship**.

### Cultural trait — Kingdom of the Two Lands

The capital gains benefits from integrating distinct regions of the kingdom. Each nome may specialize in agriculture, mining, trade, religion, war, craftsmanship, navigation or animal husbandry.

**Loyal nomes:** greater revenue · greater recruitment · more special resources · greater Prestige.
**Dissatisfied nomes may:** withhold tribute · back pretenders · refuse troops · form alliances · rebel.

### Monumentality System

The culture converts resources and labor into **Monumental Prestige**, gained through temples, obelisks, tombs, statues, palaces, great canals, recorded victories and festivals.

**Benefits:** greater legitimacy · more immigration · better diplomacy · greater Faith · reduced crisis impact.

**Risks:** excessive works consume resources · workers may revolt · granaries may empty · clans may accuse favoritism · **interrupted projects reduce Prestige**.

**Missions:** conduct the royal census · reform the nomes · modernize irrigation · **build a royal monument** · found a citadel · administer the districts · standardize currency and measures · create a diplomatic network · resolve a clan crisis · resolve a succession crisis · establish regional supremacy.

**Era risks:** destructive flood · prolonged drought · famine · administrative corruption · succession crisis · military coup · **nomarch rebellion** · civil war · **tomb desecration** · conflict between temples · epidemic · canal collapse · foreign invasion · **worker revolt** · withheld tribute · loss of Ma'at · abandoned monumental work.

## Progression Summary

| Element | Stone | Copper | Bronze | Iron |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Urban form | African Settlement | Temple settlement | City of the nome | Capital of the kingdom |
| Government | Chief and elders | Priest-ruler | Nomarch and bureaucracy | Regional kingship |
| Religion | **None** | Temple patron | Territorial cult | State religious system |
| Agriculture | Seasonal cultivation | Organized irrigation | Canal network | Advanced regional irrigation |
| Military | Communal defense | Local guard | Standing army | Regional army and chariots |
| Culture | Oral tradition | Rites and early papyrus | Writing, medicine and art | Libraries, schools and monuments |
| Expansion | Riverbanks | River routes | Control of the nome | Kingdom and subordinate territories |

**Identity of each era:** Stone, Africans Descent (see [[Descent — Africans]]) · Copper, temple and irrigation · Bronze, writing and administration · Iron, monuments and centralization.
