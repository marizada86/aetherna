# Culture — Aztecs

**Category:** [[08 - Cultures and Pantheons]] · **Descent:** Americans · **Pantheon:** [[Pantheon — Aztecs]]

Seven pillars: calpulli and lineages · intensive agriculture · markets · public religion · war and military Prestige · tribute · expansion through subordinate cities.

| Era | Aztec stage | Population | Main advance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stone | American Settlement | 20 | Descent chosen at the Gathering Point · no religion |
| Copper | Temple Settlement | 50 | Temple, market, professions and patron god |
| Bronze | **Altepetl** | 100 | Institutionalized calpulli, council, standing army and regional trade |
| Iron | Tributary Capital | 150 | Districts, tribute administration, specialized armies and subordinate cities |

## Identity

**Strengths:** intensive agriculture · efficient markets · military mobilization · prestige through victories · tribute administration · great temples · integration of religion and government · district specialization · luxury goods production · concentrated urban growth · **capacity to sustain armies through subordinate cities**.

**Weaknesses:** high religious cost · dependence on food and tribute · risk of revolt among subjugated peoples · conflicts between priests, warriors and merchants · **constant pressure for military campaigns** · vulnerability to route blockades · rivalries between calpulli · instability when the ruler loses military Prestige · need for regular festivals and ceremonies.

**Play style.** Dense cities divided into districts, sustained by markets, heavily ritualized, militarily mobilized and integrated into tribute networks.

## Core cultural system — Tonalli

In the game system, Tonalli represents **vital force, destiny, spiritual heat and accumulated Prestige** of the city and its leaders.

**Rises by:** winning battles · observing ceremonies · maintaining temples · feeding the population · holding festivals · building monuments · obtaining tribute · fulfilling favorable omens · protecting the city · maintaining the ritual calendar · honoring warriors and rulers.

**Falls with:** military defeat · famine · temple desecration · **cancelled festival** · tribute revolt · broken oaths · death of a ruler without succession · epidemic · crop failure · destruction of a monument · **ignored omen**.

| High Tonalli | Low Tonalli |
|---|---|
| Higher morale · better military performance · greater religious efficiency · more Prestige · greater loyalty · more immigration · better tribute collection · lower revolt risk | **Panic** · loss of morale · collapse of authority · revolts · **desertions** · lower production · religious conflicts · political factions · reduced tribute |

## Stone Age — defined by Descent

This culture's Stone Age **does not exist separately**. It belongs to the Americans Descent and is identical for all its cultures.

See **[[Descent — Americans]]** for the full package: founding via the Gathering Point, social organization, government by in-person voting, buildings, units and cultural trait.

Aztec identity begins only in the **Copper Age**, with the choice of the first deity. There is no religion in the Stone Age.

## Copper Age — Temple Settlement (50)

The urban center forms around temple, ceremonial square, market, ruler's house and public granary.

**Available patron gods:** Tlaloc · Mixcoatl · Tezcatlipoca.

**Government:** settlement chief assisted by priests and councilors. Institutions: ruler's house, local council, market administrator, granary supervisor, **guardian of the calendar**, guard captain, informal family representatives. Authority depends on food production, religious support, security, market prosperity, **Tonalli** and the capacity to organize ceremonies.

**Economy:** copper, maize, beans, squash, chili peppers, **cacao on a small scale**, obsidian, fish, timber, clay, fiber, **feathers**, herbs, leather, salt. Production: copper tools, obsidian blades, decorated pottery, textiles, baskets, dyes, weapons, ritual objects, **feather ornaments**, processed foods, fermented drinks, herbal remedies.

**New buildings:** adobe house, residential courtyard · **expanded milpa**, rudimentary irrigated field, food processing house · copper mine and smelter, obsidian workshop, **feather workshop**, dye house · ruler's house, public granary, **calendar house** · temple, **ritual platform**, **celestial observation post** · local market, barter square, caravan station · wood and earth wall, patrol post.

**Military:** small standing guard — spearman with shield, archer, slinger, hunter, **rudimentary macuahuitl warrior**, temple guard, scout, river patroller.

### Cultural trait — Early Tlatelolco Market

The market works as economic center, news point, **diplomatic space**, recruitment area, inspection site and food distribution center.

- **Benefits:** nearby buildings gain greater commercial efficiency, better supply, more Prestige and greater worker circulation.
- **Risks:** poorly administered markets generate theft, **counterfeit goods**, inflation, brawls, **loss of Tonalli** and popular discontent.

### Merchant System

Merchants may travel to obtain resources, discover cities, **bring information**, open routes, assess wealth and identify threats.

Successful journeys grant rare goods, Prestige, **strategic information** and new commercial relations.

**Missions:** raise the first temple · organize the market · organize the crafts · control the water · form the guard · **organize the calendar** · mediate the lineages · open a trade route · hold a great festival · discover bronze.

**Advancing:** temple · patron god · market · sustained priests · specialized professions · **organized calendar** · standing guard · bronze · 50 citizens · controlled conflicts · clans ready for seats · public festival · stable Tonalli.

## Bronze Age — Altepetl (100)

The settlement becomes an **altepetl**: a politically organized city with territory, government, temple and its own identity.

Institutionalized calpulli, clan seats and missions, formal council, urban administration, local tribute, specialized districts, standing army, great temples, regional trade and military and religious schools emerge.

**New gods:** Quetzalcoatl · Xochipilli · Xiuhtecuhtli. Those of Copper **expire** on turning the era — the choice among the three of Bronze is mandatory. Switching to another culture of the same Descent triggers the [[Deities and Passives|Wrath of the Abandoned God]].

### Calpulli

Formalized clans come to be called **calpulli**. Each calpulli is a community based on kinship, district, profession, tradition, cult, **military obligations** and land administration. See [[Clan System]].

Unlocks include a **school**, a **training ground** and **tribute responsibility**.

**Types:** agricultural · **chinampa** · mercantile · warrior · priestly · artisanal · **obsidian** · builder · fisher · messenger · aristocratic · integrated foreign.

### Government — the tlatoani

The city is governed by a **tlatoani** supported by a council and calpulli chieftains. Institutions: palace, council of the calpulli, tribunal, treasury, **tribute archive**, messengers' house, agricultural administration, market administration, ceremonial center.

| Form | Benefits | Risks |
|---|---|---|
| **Council government** | Greater district loyalty · better resource distribution · lower risk of tyranny | Slow decisions · disputes between calpulli · blocked reforms |
| **Tlatoani government** | Fast mobilization · efficient public works · better army coordination | Revolts · coups · **severe loss of legitimacy after defeats** |
| **Priestly government** | High Tonalli · more efficient festivals · greater religious stability · better omen interpretation | High cost of offerings · conflict with warriors and merchants · excessive priestly power |
| **Mercantile government** | More trade · longer routes · better access to rare resources · greater revenue | Corruption · monopolies · inequality · **foreign espionage** |

### Specialized Agriculture

**Chinampas** — where there are lakes, marshes or flooded terrain.
- *Benefits:* high production per space · frequent harvests · good integration with canals · sustained urban growth.
- *Risks:* dependence on maintenance · vulnerability to contamination · **canal blockage** · floods · sabotage.

**Calpulli lands** — each calpulli may administer part of the agricultural land, granting specialized production, tribute responsibility and greater loyalty when the lands prosper. It creates risk of land concentration, **tax evasion**, disputes between districts and **food hoarding**.

### Education

| School | Trains |
|---|---|
| **Telpochcalli** | Warriors · community workers · guards · messengers |
| **Calmecac** | Priests · administrators · astronomers · scribes · leaders |

The number of schools influences military quality, administration, religion and cultural production.

**Buildings:** tlatoani's palace, council of the calpulli, tribunal, treasury, **tribute archive**, messengers' house, great market · calpulli house, community courtyard, school, training ground, records house · bronze smelter, weapon and shield workshops, obsidian workshop, feather workshop, dye house, **chinampa**, **ritual paper workshop** · great market, merchants' house, mercantile district, collection post · stone and earth wall, **market fortress** · **early calmecac**, **telpochcalli**, observatory, music hall, **house of codices**.

**Military:** spearman, archer, slinger, **macuahuitl warrior**, shield warrior, scout, temple guard, palace guard, military messenger, veteran warrior, rudimentary siege engineer.

### Capture System

Part of the units may be trained to **capture enemies rather than kill them**.

**Captures generate:** military Prestige · information · ransoms · workers · religious advantages per the setting's rules.

**Excessive pursuit of captures can:** reduce lethal efficiency · put warriors at risk · prolong battles · **generate diplomatic backlash**.

### Signature formation — Line of Warriors

Units organized around **veteran warriors** gain morale, resistance to fear, better frontal advance, **a capture bonus** and greater cohesion.

Loses efficiency when the veteran is defeated, the line is flanked, units pursue isolated targets or terrain prevents cohesion.

### Cultural trait — Warrior Prestige

Warriors may accumulate **individual Prestige** through victories, captures, defense of the city, participation in campaigns and completion of missions.

Prestige unlocks equipment, offices, **military orders**, political influence, rewards and special missions.

**Missions:** found the altepetl · institutionalize the calpulli · create the council · **organize the tribute system** · build chinampas or advanced irrigation · form a standing army · raise a great temple · organize the great market · **create schools** · master iron.

**Advancing:** 100 citizens · calpulli seats · functioning council · calpulli missions · organized tribute · great market · standing army · great temple · **schools** · trade route · walls · iron · avoid internal war · **high Tonalli** · subordinate or ally with at least one other settlement.

## Iron Age — Tributary Capital (150)

The altepetl becomes a regional capital commanding a **tribute network** over villages, fields, chinampas, mines, markets, ports, forts, allied cities, tributary cities and conquered territories.

**New gods:** Tonatiuh · Mictlantecuhtli · Huitzilopochtli. Those of Bronze **expire** on turning the era — the choice among the three of Iron is mandatory. Switching to another culture of the same Descent triggers the [[Deities and Passives|Wrath of the Abandoned God]].

### Forms of Government

| Form | Benefits | Risks |
|---|---|---|
| **Centralizing tlatoani** | Fast campaigns · better collection · efficient great works · coordinated army | Revolts · coups · calpulli discontent · **severe crisis after military defeat** |
| **Council of nobles** | Greater elite support · better funding · lower risk of tyranny | Internal disputes · slow decisions · formation of factions |
| **Priestly government** | High Tonalli · more efficient rites · greater religious stability · better response to omens | High cost of offerings · rivalry between cults · conflict with the military · reduced civil autonomy |
| **Military government** | Stronger army · fast recruitment · more efficient expansion · greater war Prestige | Constant campaigns · high military cost · coups · **loss of legitimacy in peacetime** |
| **Tributary government** | More resources · efficient markets · greater tribute · better logistics | Corruption · excessive taxation · provincial revolts · lower military morale |

**Economy:** regional taxation, **tribute warehouses**, mint or exchange standard, market inspection, customs, long-distance merchants, military and tribute routes, subordinate markets, monopolies, public food distribution.

**Buildings:** fortified palace, great council, central tribunal, treasury, **tribute archive**, **house of codices**, messengers' house, embassy, **ministry of the market**, **ministry of tribute** · expanded seat, calpulli palace, **district fortress**, lineage archive, own school · iron mine, **advanced chinampas**, advanced irrigation, feather workshop, textile complex, **codex workshop** · great regional market, customs, **tribute warehouse**, credit house, **elevated causeways** · fortified wall, citadel, military academy, forts along the routes, **messenger posts** · aqueduct, cisterns, **canal network**, house of healing, **baths or sweat houses**, urban causeways · **higher calmecac**, expanded telpochcalli, house of codices, **school of astronomers**, **botanical garden**, music hall.

**Military:** macuahuitl warrior, iron spearman, veteran archer, veteran slinger, shield warrior, temple guard, palace guard, elite warrior, scout, military messenger, engineer, siege unit, general, urban guard.

### Warrior Orders

| Order | Favors |
|---|---|
| **Eagle Warriors** | Mobility · Prestige · fast strikes · capture of commanders |
| **Jaguar Warriors** | Melee combat · infiltration · morale · **night attacks** |
| **Warriors of the Sun** | Temple defense · religious morale · resilience · war against marked enemies |

### Cultural trait — Tribute Network

The capital may demand tribute from subordinate cities: food, textiles, obsidian, metals, feathers, timber, **soldiers**, workers and luxury goods.

**High loyalty:** more tribute · lower escort cost · more troops · better trade.
**Dissatisfied cities may:** delay tribute · **send inferior goods** · sabotage routes · support enemies · **form coalitions** · rebel.

### Ritual and Political Warfare System

War may have distinct objectives: territorial conquest · obtaining tribute · **capture of warriors** · punishment of rebellion · route control · demonstration of power · religious defense.

**Successful campaigns grant:** Tonalli · Prestige · tribute · experience · legitimacy.
**Failed campaigns cause:** loss of Tonalli · revolts · questioning of the tlatoani · military rivalries · reduced tribute.

### Codex System

Codices record tribute, families, calpulli, laws, calendar, wars, genealogies, omens, routes and subordinate cities.

**They increase:** administrative efficiency · tribute control · research · legitimacy · historical memory.

**The destruction of codices causes:** loss of information · **tribute confusion** · territorial disputes · succession crises.

**Missions:** conduct the great census · reform the council · expand chinampas and irrigation · **organize the tribute network** · build the monumental temple · **form warrior orders** · administer the districts · standardize markets and measures · create a diplomatic network · organize a regional festival · resolve a crisis between calpulli · **suppress or negotiate a revolt** · establish regional supremacy.

**Era risks:** tribute revolt · war between calpulli · succession crisis · military coup · **priestly conspiracy** · famine · **chinampa failure** · water contamination · epidemic · trade blockade · military defeat · loss of Tonalli · **coalition of enemy cities** · tribute corruption · unsupplied market · **rebellion of a warrior order** · destruction of codices · temple desecration.
