# Culture — Yoruba

**Category:** [[08 - Cultures and Pantheons]] · **Descent:** Africans · **Pantheon:** [[Pantheon — Yoruba]]

Six pillars: lineages and extended families · worship of the orishas and ancestors · community prestige · markets and professional specialization · councils and political balance · the relationship between city, territory and spiritual forces.

The central characteristic is the transformation of a community organized by kinship into a politically complex city sustained by markets, priesthoods, professional guilds, councils and royal authority.

| Era | Yoruba stage | Population | Main advance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stone | African Settlement | 20 | Descent chosen at the Gathering Point · no religion |
| Copper | Worship Settlement | 50 | Temple, market, professions and patron orisha |
| Bronze | City of the Council | 100 | Clan seats, own missions, guilds, walls and formal government |
| Iron | Regional Yoruba Kingdom | 150 | Districts, palace, political houses, subordinate cities and regional expansion |

## Identity

**Strengths:** efficient markets · diversified agriculture · metallurgy and craftsmanship · **high communal cohesion** · religion integrated into urban life · healing and disease control · diplomacy between lineages · cultural production · decentralized administration · adaptation across forest, savanna and rivers · strong professional specialization.

**Weaknesses:** rivalries between lineages · disputes between political and religious authorities · the need to balance many social groups · risk of fragmentation between districts and houses · dependence on markets to distribute resources · conflicts between cults · complex political succession · the possibility of subordinate cities challenging the center · **severe penalties when taboos are violated**.

**Play style.** Dense cities organized into districts, economically specialized, religiously diverse, **governed by negotiation** and sustained by markets and community networks.

## Core cultural system — Àṣẹ

A cultural resource unique to the culture. In the game system, Àṣẹ represents **the spiritual, social and ritual capacity to turn intention into realization**.

**Rises by:** observing rites · honoring ancestors · keeping markets supplied · resolving disputes · **respecting taboos** · protecting sacred sites · maintaining priests and healers · keeping promises · holding festivals · governing with legitimacy · integrating different lineages.

**Falls with:** desecration of shrines · broken oaths · **violation of taboos** · abandonment of the dead · famine · unanswered epidemics · ritual corruption · war between lineages · a government deemed illegitimate · indiscriminate destruction of nature · failure at important ceremonies.

| High Àṣẹ | Low Àṣẹ |
|---|---|
| Greater religious efficiency · better morale · greater artisanal productivity · lower risk of internal conflict · greater healer effectiveness · more Prestige · better diplomacy · greater lineage loyalty | Spiritual conflicts · disease · loss of morale · production failures · rivalries · questioning of the ruler · reduced religious effectiveness · rising crime and sabotage · **negative omens** |

## Stone Age — defined by Descent

This culture's Stone Age **does not exist separately**. It belongs to the Africans Descent and is identical for all its cultures.

See **[[Descent — Africans]]** for the full package: founding via the Gathering Point, social organization, government by in-person voting, buildings, units and cultural trait.

Yoruba identity begins only in the **Copper Age**, with the choice of the first deity. There is no religion in the Stone Age.

## Copper Age — Worship Settlement (50)

The urban center forms around market, temple, ceremonial square, ruler's house and family courtyards.

**Available patron orishas:** Oxóssi · Oxum · Obaluaê/Omolu.

**Government:** settlement chieftain supported by elders, priests and lineage representatives. Institutions: chief's house, local council, **audience courtyard**, market administrator, **guardian of customs**, granary supervisor, lineage spokespeople. Authority depends on negotiating capacity, market prosperity, religious respect, resource distribution, family support and **maintenance of Àṣẹ**.

**Economy:** copper, tubers, grain, **vegetable oils**, fish, timber, clay, leather, fiber, medicinal herbs, dyes, honey, fruit, nuts. Production: copper tools, decorated pottery, textiles, baskets, dyes, oils, **beads and ornaments**, copper weapons, herbal remedies, processed foods, ritual objects.

**New buildings:** mud house, **residential courtyard** · expanded swidden field, medicinal garden, food processing house · copper mine and smelter, early smithy, **bead and ornament workshop**, **dye house** · central square, audience courtyard, registry post · temple, sacred grove, **ritual healing house**, ancestral altar · local market, **barter square**, caravan station · wood and earth wall, patrol post.

**Military:** small standing guard — spearman with shield, archer, hunter, scout, temple guard, copper sword or machete warrior, **forest patroller**, sailor or river guard.

### Cultural trait — Living Market

The market is the settlement's economic **and social** center: place of trade, space for news, point of negotiation, venue for political encounters, distribution center and festival ground.

- **Benefits:** nearby buildings gain greater resource circulation, more Prestige, better access to workers and greater satisfaction.
- **Risks:** unsupplied or unsafe markets cause loss of morale, **inflation**, theft, conflicts, loss of Prestige and **reduced Àṣẹ**.

### Lineage Specialization

Each lineage may become known for agriculture, hunting, fishing, trade, healing, priesthood, craftsmanship, war, mining or transport.

Specialization grants economic bonuses but increases disputes over resources, space, recognition, the ruler's favor and **influence over the temple**.

**Missions:** raise the first temple · organize the market · organize the crafts · **protect the water sources** · form the guard · **create a house of healing** · mediate the lineages · open a trade route · **hold the first great festival** · discover bronze.

**Advancing:** temple · patron orisha · market · specialized professions · standing guard · **house of healing** · controlled conflicts between lineages · bronze · 50 citizens · clans ready for seats · **public festival held** · stable Àṣẹ.

## Bronze Age — City of the Council (100)

Clan seats and missions, formal councils, professional guilds, specialized districts, recognized laws, taxation, standing army, regional trade, great temples and urban administration emerge.

**New orishas:** Ogum · Iansã/Oyá · Exu. Those of Copper **expire** on turning the era — the choice among the three of Bronze is mandatory. Switching to another culture of the same Descent triggers the [[Deities and Passives|Wrath of the Abandoned God]].

### Clans

Yoruba types: agricultural · **hunters** · mercantile · **smith** · artisanal · priestly · **healer** · warrior · **messengers** · river · aristocratic · integrated foreign. See [[Clan System]].

### Professional Guilds

Beyond clans, professions may form guilds: smiths · potters · weavers · **sculptors** · merchants · hunters · healers · builders · messengers · boatmen.

They may control quality standards, train apprentices, negotiate prices, request privileges, fund construction and **come into conflict with clans**.

### Forms of Government

The ruler must share authority with the council of elders, clan chieftains, district chiefs, priests and guild representatives.

| Form | Benefits | Risks |
|---|---|---|
| **Council of lineages** | Greater clan loyalty · better dispute resolution · lower risk of authoritarianism | Slow decisions · rivalry between houses · smaller lineages without representation |
| **Limited kingship** | Better coordination · more efficient public works · greater legitimacy when the councils support the ruler | Conflict between ruler and council · succession crisis · attempts at excessive centralization |
| **Priestly government** | Greater Àṣẹ · more efficient festivals · less impact from spiritual crises · better disease control | High religious cost · conflicts between cults · lower civil autonomy |
| **Mercantile government** | Efficient market · more revenue · expanded trade routes · cheaper mercantile buildings | Inequality · corruption · dissatisfaction among farmers and warriors · monopolies |

**Buildings:** palace or royal house, council of lineages, tribunal, public archive, messengers' house, great market · clan seat, clan courtyard, lineage house · bronze smelter, smithy, sculptors' workshop, **dye house**, **bead workshop** · great market, caravan house, mercantile district · rammed-earth wall (or stone where material allows), monumental gate · **house of stories**, apprentice school, **music hall**, ancestral monument, house of healing, priest school, **judgment courtyard**.

**Military:** spearman, archer, sword warrior, shield warrior, veteran hunter, palace guard, temple guard, scout, **mounted messenger or runner**, river patroller, rudimentary siege engineer.

### Signature formation — Circle of Shields

Units organized around a central point gain defense against flanking, greater morale, **better protection of priests and commanders** and resistance to knockback.

Loses efficiency when dispersed, attacked by intense fire, prevented from holding the center or moved quickly.

### Cultural trait — City of Quarters

Possible specialized districts: smiths · merchants · priests · artisans · hunters · healers · military · foreign.

Each district gains bonuses when it has compatible buildings, specialized workers, **a related shrine** and a good link to the market.

**Neglected districts generate:** criminality · rivalry · strikes · disease · loss of loyalty.

### Mediation System

Conflicts may be resolved by compensation, oath, elder mediation, public judgment, priestly intervention, political marriage or distribution of offices.

**Resolving disputes without violence grants Àṣẹ, Prestige, loyalty and better diplomacy.**

**Missions:** found the council · recognize the clans · organize the districts · record customs and laws · form a standing army · expand the great market · raise a great temple · **control an epidemic** · build a regional road · master iron.

**Advancing:** 100 citizens · clan seats · functioning council · clan missions · organized districts · great market · walls · standing army · trade route · sustained religious system · iron · avoid war between lineages · **stable Àṣẹ and legitimacy**.

## Iron Age — Regional Yoruba Kingdom (150)

The capital exerts influence over villages, fields, forests, mines, river ports, subordinate markets, allied cities, tributary cities and conquered territories.

**New orishas:** Xangô · Oxalá · Iemanjá. Those of Bronze **expire** on turning the era — the choice among the three of Iron is mandatory. Switching to another culture of the same Descent triggers the [[Deities and Passives|Wrath of the Abandoned God]].

### Forms of Government

| Form | Benefits | Risks |
|---|---|---|
| **Kingship limited by the council** | High legitimacy · better balance between houses · lower risk of tyranny · greater internal stability | Slow decisions · institutional conflicts · **councils may block reforms** |
| **Centralized kingship** | Faster works · coordinated army · better tribute collection · fast crisis response | Revolts · loss of lineage support · coups · sharp fall in legitimacy |
| **Government of the houses** | More private investment · greater elite loyalty · better local administration | Fragmentation · rivalries · subordinate cities controlled by ambitious families |
| **Priestly government** | High Àṣẹ · better disease control · more efficient festivals and temples · lower risk of spiritual crises | Competing cults · high cost of offerings · conflicts with military and merchants |
| **Mercantile government** | Greater trade · more currency · expanded routes · more profitable tributary cities | Corruption · inequality · monopolies · discontent among farmers and warriors |

**Economy:** mint, formal taxation, customs, great markets, mercantile credit, guilds, tribute from subordinate cities, land and river routes, regional markets, **granted monopolies**, state warehouses.

**Buildings:** royal palace, **great audience courtyard**, higher council, central tribunal, royal archive, mint, embassy, **chieftains' hall**, messengers' house · expanded seat, **lineage palace**, clan market, lineage archive, **guild house** · iron mine, primitive blast furnace, great forge, advanced irrigation, water mill, textile complex, **dye workshop** · great regional market, credit house, customs, **mercantile guild** · fortified wall, citadel, border and river forts, monumental gates · public wells, cisterns, house of healing, **religious hospital**, public squares · priest school, **healer school**, house of stories, **archive of traditions**, academy of arts, music hall, **great festival square**.

**Military:** iron spearman, veteran archer, sword warrior, shield warrior, royal guard, temple guard, veteran hunter, scout, military messenger, river patroller, engineer, siege unit, general, urban guard.

**Possible special units:** **Guard of Thunder** (Xangô) · **Guardian of the Forge** (Ogum) · **Forest Scout** (Oxóssi) · Priest-healer · **Messenger of Exu** · **Water Patroller** (Iemanjá).

### Signature formation — Wall of Courtyards

Units organize into defensive groups around commanders, priests or objectives.

**Benefits:** defense against attacks from multiple directions · high morale · protection of support units · better bonus sharing · **greater resilience in urban combat**.

Loses efficiency when the center is eliminated, units become isolated, the group is forced to pursue enemies or long-range attacks prevent concentration.

### Cultural trait — Network of Cities

The capital may integrate subordinate cities, each specialized in agriculture, mining, trade, religion, craftsmanship, war, healing or navigation.

**Loyal cities:** greater revenue · more resources sent · more troops · more routes opened · greater regional Prestige.
**Dissatisfied cities may:** withhold tribute · back pretenders · **close markets** · refuse troops · form alliances · rebel.

### Regional Festival System

Great festivals attract merchants, priests, artists, diplomats, pilgrims and foreign rulers.

**They grant:** Àṣẹ · Prestige · trade · loyalty · diplomacy · urban growth.
**Poorly organized, they cause:** famine · accidents · disease · conflicts between cults · loss of Prestige.

### Succession

May depend on lineage, council approval, religious support, prestige, military backing and balance between houses.

**A contested succession generates:** factions · coup attempts · territorial division · religious intervention · war between lineages.

**Missions:** conduct the census · reform the council · modernize agriculture · build regional infrastructure · found the citadel · administer the districts · standardize currency and measures · create a diplomatic network · **organize a regional festival** · resolve a crisis between houses · resolve the succession · establish regional supremacy.

**Era risks:** war between houses · succession crisis · coup · rebellion of a subordinate city · corruption · conflict between cults · epidemic · famine · urban fire · mercantile monopoly · **guild revolt** · **breach of taboos** · loss of Àṣẹ · invasion · closure of routes · usurpation · fragmentation of the kingdom.
