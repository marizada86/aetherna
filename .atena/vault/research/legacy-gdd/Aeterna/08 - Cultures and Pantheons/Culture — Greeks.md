# Culture — Greeks

**Category:** [[08 - Cultures and Pantheons]] · **Descent:** Westerners · **Pantheon:** [[Pantheon — Greeks]]

Greek culture develops around five pillars: formation of the polis · citizenship and political participation · worship of the gods · military specialization · philosophy, art and knowledge.

Its main characteristic is the gradual transformation of a community of families and clans into a politically organized city.

| Era | Greek stage | Population | Main advance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stone | Western Settlement | 20 | Descent chosen at the Gathering Point · no religion |
| Copper | Cult Settlement | 50 | Temple, early agora, professions and patron god |
| Bronze | Polis | 100 | Acropolis, clan seats, citizenship, hoplites and clan missions |
| Iron | Hellenic Power | 150 | Districts, democracy or aristocracy, colonies, philosophy and naval expansion |

## Identity

**Strengths:** urban administration · culture and Prestige · organized infantry · maritime trade · philosophy and research · monumental construction · diplomacy between cities.

**Weaknesses:** rivalry between clans · political conflict · dependence on free citizens · disputes between religious cult and government · limited agricultural territory · risk of division into factions · high cost of monumental construction.

**Play style.** Favors compact, specialized and politically complex cities. The player must balance traditional clans, citizens, priests, merchants, soldiers, philosophers, resident foreigners and the rural population. Greek power depends not only on resources but on Prestige, political participation, military discipline, religious recognition and cultural influence.

## Stone Age — defined by Descent

This culture's Stone Age **does not exist separately**. It belongs to the Westerners Descent and is identical for all its cultures.

See **[[Descent — Westerners]]** for the full package: founding via the Gathering Point, social organization, government by in-person voting, buildings, units and cultural trait.

Greek identity begins only in the **Copper Age**, with the choice of the first deity. There is no religion in the Stone Age.

## Copper Age — Cult Settlement (50)

Specialized artisans, permanent farmers, specialized herders, miners, merchants, priests, guards and builders emerge. The urban center begins to form around a public square and a temple.

**Available patron gods:** Demeter · Artemis · Apollo.

**Government:** local council led by an **archon**. Institutions: council house, archon, council of elders, limited assembly, resource registrar, market administrator. The assembly lets influential families weigh in on decisions, but formal citizenship does not yet exist.

**Institutional religion:** temple, priesthood, patron god, religious festivals, public offerings, ritual calendar, minor shrines.

**Economy:** copper, grain, olives, grapes, fish, wool, pottery, timber, stone, medicinal herbs. Characteristic production: rudimentary wine, rudimentary olive oil, painted pottery, copper tools, wool textiles, fishing boats, religious figurines.

**New buildings:** early olive grove, early vineyard, fishing port, manual press · copper mine and smelter, pottery workshop, weaving, carpentry, quarry · archon's house, public square, public warehouse · temple, priests' house, shrine, ceremonial square, rudimentary oracle · local market, trading post, dock, caravan station.

**Military:** small standing guard — spearman with shield, archer, slinger, hunter, temple guard, armed sailor, scout. Still **no** complete hoplite formation.

### Cultural trait — First Agora

The public square works simultaneously as market, political space, religious center, festival ground and citizens' meeting place.

- **Benefits:** administrative, commercial and religious buildings near the square gain additional efficiency.
- **Risks:** negative events in the square quickly affect Order, Trade, Faith and Prestige.

**Missions:** raise the first temple · form the agora · organize the crafts · develop oil and wine · form the guard · mediate the clans · open a sea route · discover bronze.

**Advancing:** temple · patron god · agora · specialized professions · guard · maritime or regional trade · bronze · 50 citizens · clans ready to build seats.

## Bronze Age — Polis (100)

The settlement becomes a **polis**: a politically organized city formed by the urban center and its agricultural territory. Citizenship, acropolis, clan seats, clan missions, formal government, written laws, standing army, phalanx, regional trade, great temples and cultural production emerge.

**New gods:** Ares · Athena · Hephaestus. Those of Copper **expire** on turning the era — the choice among the three of Bronze is mandatory. Switching to another culture of the same Descent triggers the [[Deities and Passives|Wrath of the Abandoned God]].

### Citizenship

**Grants:** right of political participation · right to serve in the army · legal protection · prestige · access to public office.

**Requires:** military service · payment of tribute · participation in festivals · defense of the polis · compliance with the laws.

### Clans

Greek types: agricultural · mercantile · maritime · warrior · priestly · artisanal · aristocratic · **philosophers** · builders · integrated foreign. See [[Clan System]].

### Forms of Government

| Form | Benefits | Risks |
|---|---|---|
| **Aristocracy** — power of the main clans | Loyalty of wealthy clans · better funding of buildings · accessible elite troops | Lower popular satisfaction · smaller clans without influence · succession disputes |
| **Oligarchy** — power of the wealthiest citizens | Efficient trade · greater revenue · strong mercantile buildings | Inequality · corruption · discontent among farmers and soldiers |
| **Tyranny** — centralized power | Fast construction · immediate decisions · greater military control | Revolts · political assassinations · clan resistance |
| **Early democracy** — citizen assemblies | High satisfaction · stability when decisions pass · greater cultural production | Slow decisions · factional disputes · exclusion of foreigners and non-citizens |

**Economy:** wine, olive oil, decorated pottery, bronze weapons, armor, statues, textiles, salted fish, naval timber, religious objects.

**Buildings:** agora, acropolis, palace or prytaneion, tribunal, public archive, treasury, magistrates' house · clan seat and annexes · bronze smelter, weapon and armor workshops, shipyard, great pottery works, olive press, winery, irrigated farm, advanced quarry, sculptors' workshop · early theater, gymnasium, school of scribes, artistic workshop, festival hall, public monument · stone wall, towers, monumental gate, barracks, arsenal, military camp, stable, military port.

**Military:** hoplite, veteran hoplite, peltast, archer, slinger, light cavalry, acropolis guard, war sailor, ship crew, rudimentary siege engineer.

### Signature formation — Hoplite Phalanx

Adjacent hoplites gain additional defense, resistance to frontal attacks, resistance to knockback and greater spear efficiency.

Loses efficiency when surrounded, attacked on the flanks, moved over uneven terrain or disorganized.

### Cultural trait — Pride of the Polis

Citizens gain bonuses when defending the agora, acropolis, patron temple, walls and clan seats. Defensive victories grant Prestige, Faith, loyalty and military experience. **Defeats inside the city cause sharp loss of morale and Prestige.**

**Missions:** found the polis · recognize the clans · institute citizenship · form the phalanx · record the laws · celebrate the games · open a regional sea route · balance the clans · raise a great temple · discover iron.

**Advancing:** 100 citizens · seats for the main clans · functioning government · clan missions completed · stone walls · standing army · trade route · acropolis · recorded laws · iron metallurgy · avoid civil war.

## Iron Age — Hellenic Power (150)

The polis exerts influence over villages, ports, colonies, sea routes, allied cities and conquered territories. Politics requires balancing assembly, magistrates, generals, priests, philosophers, merchants, aristocratic clans, common citizens, foreigners and subordinate territories.

**New gods:** Zeus · Poseidon · Hades. Those of Bronze **expire** on turning the era — the choice among the three of Iron is mandatory. Switching to another culture of the same Descent triggers the [[Deities and Passives|Wrath of the Abandoned God]].

**Government:** the four forms become complete and reformable — Democracy (assembly, culture, resistance to tyranny; risks: slowness, demagoguery, factionalism) · Oligarchy (revenue, trade, fleets; risks: inequality, corruption, revolts) · Aristocracy (better generals, veteran troops; risks: dynastic disputes, political exclusion) · Tyranny (immediate decisions, accelerated construction; risks: rebellions, assassinations, clan coalitions).

**Economy:** mint, formal taxation, customs, mercantile credit, maritime trade, commercial colonies, taxation of subordinate cities, market for strategic resources.

**Buildings:** bouleuterion, ekklesia, central tribunal, prytaneion, mint, embassy, census office, district administration · academy, library, theater, gymnasium, stadium, philosophical school, engineering workshop, museum or temple of the muses · great agora, commercial and military ports, customs, mercantile district, credit house, naval warehouse, colonial trading post · fortified wall, citadel, military academy, central arsenal, military shipyard, coastal and border forts · aqueduct, cistern, baths, house of healing, firefighting corps, paved roads, stone bridges.

**Military:** iron hoplite, veteran hoplite, sacred guard, veteran peltast, specialized archer, cavalry, elite cavalry, war sailor, engineer, siege unit, general, urban guard. **Naval:** merchant ship, exploration ship, trireme, transport ship, blockade ship, colonization vessel.

### Cultural trait — Hellenic Rivalry

The city gains Prestige by surpassing other poleis in war, trade, philosophy, architecture, festivals, games and navigation.

The player may declare an **area of excellence** — military, commercial, cultural, religious, naval or philosophical. Specialization grants relevant bonuses but **increases rivalry with competing cities**.

### Philosophy

A system unique to Greek culture. Possible schools:

| School | Favors |
|---|---|
| Rational | Research, engineering, administration |
| Civic | Order, citizenship, diplomacy |
| Military | Discipline, strategy, formation |
| Natural | Agriculture, medicine, knowledge of the environment |
| Rhetorical | Politics, prestige, negotiation |

### Colonization

**Functions:** obtain resources · control routes · expand influence · create ports · relieve population pressure · project military power.

**Risks:** rebellion · administrative distance · naval attacks · rivalry with local peoples · disputes between colonizing clans.

**Missions:** reform the government · conduct the census · build a philosophical center · create a fleet · found a colony · celebrate Panhellenic games · build a monument · resolve a clan crisis · lead a league · achieve hegemony.

**Era risks:** civil war · political coup · demagoguery · tyranny · colonial rebellion · rivalry between poleis · naval blockade · corruption · religious conflict · urban epidemics · supply crisis · succession dispute.

## Progression Summary

| Element | Stone | Copper | Bronze | Iron |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Urban form | Western Settlement | Settlement | Polis | Hellenic power |
| Government | Chief and elders | Archon and council | Formal government | Democracy, oligarchy, aristocracy or tyranny |
| Clans | Informal | Economic and religious | Institutionalized | Aristocratic houses and factions |
| Religion | **None** | Temple patron | Cult of the polis | Panhellenic system |
| Urban center | Gathering Point | Public square | Agora and acropolis | Civic and monumental districts |
| Military | Communal defense | Guard | Phalanx | Regional army and fleet |
| Culture | Oral tradition | Festivals | Theater and gymnasium | Philosophy, academy and games |
| Expansion | Survival | Regional contact | Influence of the polis | Colonies and hegemony |

**Identity of each era:** Stone, Westerners Descent (see [[Descent — Westerners]]) · Copper, cult and specialization · Bronze, polis and citizenship · Iron, philosophy and hegemony.
