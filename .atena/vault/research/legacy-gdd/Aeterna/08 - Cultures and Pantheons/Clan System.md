# Clan System

**Category:** [[08 - Cultures and Pantheons]]

A clan is a social group formed by one or more families sharing identity, kinship, traditions, interests or common origin. **Clans exist from the Stone Age**, but only become formal institutions in Bronze. See [[Era Progression]].

Each citizen may belong to a clan.

## Elements of a Clan

Name · Symbol · Member families · Informal or recognized leader · Traditions · Predominant profession · Religious preference · Relations with other clans · Prestige level · Loyalty to the city · Own interests.

## Evolution by Era

### Stone Age

Clans **can** influence occupations, personal relations, marriages, religious preferences, cooperation in production, support for the chief and rivalries between families.

Clans **cannot** build seats, offer missions, hold official territory, keep their own troops or sit in a formal council.

Members live in common huts, family houses or communal houses — there is no seat.

### Copper Age

They gain economic and religious influence. A clan may become known for agriculture, mining, craftsmanship, trade, war, priesthood, animal husbandry or seafaring, and compete for prestige, resources and the ruler's support.

They still have **no** seat of their own, exclusive missions, official representation, formal territory or exclusive troops.

### Bronze Age — Institutionalization

The system's turning point. Each clan may now:

build a seat · appoint a recognized chieftain · offer missions · request resources · fund construction · support political decisions · oppose the ruler · form alliances · create rivalries · sponsor troops · control a profession · influence a district · fund temples · accumulate wealth and prestige.

**Possible clan types:** agricultural · mercantile · warrior · priestly · mining · artisanal · maritime · aristocratic · foreign · animal breeders.

**Relations between clans:** alliance · rivalry · political marriage · economic dispute · religious dispute · commercial dependence · vassalage · neutrality · historical enmity · military cooperation.

**Clan buildings:** seat · meeting hall · warehouse · workshop · shrine · courtyard. The seat is the central building; the others may require upgrades or specializations of it.

### Iron Age — Regional Power

A clan may become a noble house, control a district, take on a military function, lead a guild, form a political faction, control temples, administer rural areas, become a vassal, rebel, split into smaller clans, absorb rivals, form a dynasty or control subordinate cities.

**Expanded clan buildings:** enlarged seat · clan palace · clan fortress · clan market · clan temple · lineage archive · clan barracks · rural estate · specialized workshop.

## Clan Missions

Unlocked in the Bronze Age. They represent the clan's specific interests, not the city's.

| Clan type | Mission examples |
|---|---|
| Agricultural | Expand fields · build canals · secure seed · protect a harvest · reduce agricultural taxes |
| Mercantile | Open a new route · build a warehouse · escort a caravan · reduce fees · secure exclusivity over a product |
| Warrior | Build a training ground · equip warriors · eliminate bandits · fortify a border · lead a campaign |
| Priestly | Build a shrine · organize a festival · recover a relic · convert citizens · fight a rival cult |
| Mining | Find a new deposit · build a mine · improve safety · increase output · defend an ore route |

In the **Iron Age** missions become political: control of districts, succession disputes, dynastic marriages, land grants, trade monopolies, military commands, expeditions, temple construction, backing candidates, rebellions, investigations, political assassinations, negotiations between families and administration of subordinate cities.

### Possible Rewards

Prestige · resources · workers · troops · cost reduction · production bonus · political support · new technologies · special buildings · improved relations.

### Consequences of Refusal

Loss of prestige · reduced loyalty · rivalry · strike · sabotage · support for another ruler · conflict with another clan · reduced production.

## Relation to the Rest of the GDD

Several divine passives in [[Deities and Passives]] act directly on clans — Tyr conditions bonuses on not breaking clan promises, Zeus rewards loyal clans, Xangô punishes clans that break laws, Tau enriches ambitious clans at the cost of corruption.

The Bronze Age **council of clans** and the Iron Age **senate/higher council** need reconciling with [[The Town Hall and the Council]], which describes a Council elected among players. They are potentially the same body under different names.
