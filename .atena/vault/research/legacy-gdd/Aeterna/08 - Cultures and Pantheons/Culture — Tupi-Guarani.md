# Culture — Tupi-Guarani

**Category:** [[08 - Cultures and Pantheons]] · **Descent:** Americans · **Pantheon:** [[Pantheon — Tupi-Guarani]]

> **Cultural adaptation note (from the source).** This structure is an **internal interpretation of the setting**. The term "Tupi-Guarani" covers different peoples, territories, periods and traditions, and the playable culture **must not be presented as a uniform historical reconstruction**.
>
> The systems combine elements of communal organization, extended families, villages, agriculture, hunting and fishing, territorial mobility, spiritual relationship with the forest/rivers/ancestors, alliances between communities, oral tradition, war and diplomacy.
>
> **Specific terms need later revision** according to the peoples that will serve as canonical reference for the project.

Seven pillars: extended families and kinship groups · communal life · agriculture integrated with the forest · hunting, fishing and gathering · territorial mobility · oral tradition · spiritual relationship with nature.

| Era | Cultural stage | Population | Main advance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stone | American Settlement | 20 | Descent chosen at the Gathering Point · no religion |
| Copper | Structured **Tekoá** | 50 | Ceremonial house, organized cultivation, professions and patron entity |
| Bronze | Great Confederated Village | 100 | Clan seats, own missions, formal council and alliances |
| Iron | Regional Dominion of Villages | 150 (capital) | Districts, territorial network, great expeditions and regional influence |

## Identity

**Strengths:** mobility in forests · exploration · hunting and fishing · plant knowledge · diversified food production · **camouflage** · diplomacy between communities · **low environmental impact** · recovery of natural resources · oral tradition · family cohesion · territorial adaptation.

**Weaknesses:** lower efficiency in extremely dense cities · difficulty sustaining monumental structures · dependence on forest, rivers and natural cycles · **vulnerability to deforestation** · family rivalries · fragmentation between villages · difficulty of political centralization · lower large-scale metal production · risk of cultural loss during excessive expansion · **need to relocate exhausted productive areas**.

**Play style.** Settlements integrated with the environment, distributed, connected by trails and rivers, organized around communal spaces, dependent on family cooperation and **able to shift part of their productive structure**.

## Core cultural system — Teko

In the game system, Teko represents **the community's way of life**: customs, social relations, spiritual harmony, family organization, balanced use of territory and continuity of traditions.

**Rises by:** keeping families united · **protecting forests and rivers** · observing rites · respecting sacred areas · resolving disputes · sharing food · maintaining allied villages · keeping promises · preserving oral tradition · avoiding excessive exploitation · holding celebrations.

**Falls with:** famine · destruction of sacred areas · river contamination · war between families · broken alliances · abandonment of the ancestors · **excessive exploitation** · epidemic · **authoritarian government** · forced displacement · loss of traditions · severe inequality.

| High Teko | Low Teko |
|---|---|
| Higher morale · better communal productivity · **greater environmental regeneration** · better diplomacy · lower conflict risk · greater spiritual efficiency · more village loyalty · better territorial adaptation | Rivalries · **migration** · **abandonment of the settlement** · reduced production · spiritual conflicts · loss of loyalty · disease · clan division · village rebellion |

## Environmental system — Balance of the Territory

Each region has a level of **Environmental Balance**, influenced by the number of trees, availability of animals, water quality, soil fertility, intensity of gathering, number of plantations, presence of sacred areas and population density.

| High Balance | Low Balance |
|---|---|
| More game · more fish · more medicinal plants · better fertility · lower disease incidence · more Teko · **better camouflage** | Scarcity · animal migration · erosion · water contamination · disease · falling Teko · **need to move fields or settlements** |

The culture is **not prevented** from exploiting resources, but must rotate productive areas and let used regions recover.

## Stone Age — defined by Descent

This culture's Stone Age **does not exist separately**. It belongs to the Americans Descent and is identical for all its cultures.

See **[[Descent — Americans]]** for the full package: founding via the Gathering Point, social organization, government by in-person voting, buildings, units and cultural trait.

Tupi-Guarani identity begins only in the **Copper Age**, with the choice of the first deity. There is no religion in the Stone Age.

## Copper Age — Structured Tekoá (50)

The community becomes a **tekoá**: in the game system, the space where the community develops its way of life, bringing together dwellings, fields, forests, rivers, spiritual sites, paths and gathering spaces.

**Available patron entities:** Cairé/Rairé · Caipora/Curupira · Guaraci.

**Government:** recognized leader assisted by an informal council. Institutions: leader's house, council of elders, **audience courtyard**, family spokespeople, **guardian of the territory**, keeper of the storehouse, **chief messenger**. Authority depends on Prestige, **generosity**, negotiating capacity, territorial knowledge, family support, **Teko** and success in crises.

**Economy:** copper, cassava, maize, sweet potato, fruit, fish, meat, timber, clay, fiber, feathers, herbs, honey, leather, dyes. Production: **cassava flour**, copper tools, decorated pottery, baskets, nets, textiles, dyes, **canoes**, weapons, herbal remedies, ornaments, preserved foods.

**New buildings:** expanded communal house, residential courtyard, visitors' shelter · organized swidden field, cassava field, **cassava processing house**, smokehouse · copper mine and smelter, **canoe workshop**, dye house, herb house · council house, gathering square, **messengers' house** · **ceremonial house**, sacred clearing, **protected grove**, house of healing, ancestral marker · barter square, **canoe landing**, trade trail · reinforced palisade, observation platform.

**Military:** small standing guard — archer, spearman, hunter, forest scout, club warrior, **ceremonial guard**, river patroller, messenger.

### Cultural trait — Living Tekoá

**The city is not made only of buildings.** Its functional territory includes fields, forests, rivers, sacred sites, trails, hunting grounds, orchards and the settlement itself.

- **Benefits:** the more balanced these elements, the higher the Teko, the better the production, the more natural resources appear and the greater the resilience to crises.
- **Risks:** an **excessively built** city loses fertility, game, camouflage, herbs and Teko.

### Family Specialization

Each family or clan may become known for agriculture, hunting, fishing, healing, craftsmanship, pottery, war, river navigation, spiritual leadership or diplomacy.

Specialization grants bonuses but increases disputes over prestige, resources, recognition, space and **influence over the leader**.

**Missions:** build the ceremonial house · **organize the tekoá** · organize barter · organize the crafts · form the guard · **build a canoe fleet** · create a house of healing · mediate the families · open a trade trail · **hold a great celebration** · discover bronze.

**Advancing:** ceremonial house · patron entity · organized productive areas · **forest and water preserved** · barter square · professions · standing guard · house of healing · bronze · 50 citizens · controlled conflicts · clans ready for seats · **contact with another village** · stable Teko.

## Bronze Age — Great Confederated Village (100)

The tekoá becomes a great village that **coordinates smaller communities**. Clan seats and missions, formal council, alliances between villages, territorial administration, specialized production, standing army, regional routes, great celebrations and **messenger networks** emerge.

**New entities:** Tau · Angatupyry · Itaguará. Those of Copper **expire** on turning the era — the choice among the three of Bronze is mandatory. Switching to another culture of the same Descent triggers the [[Deities and Passives|Wrath of the Abandoned God]].

### Clans

Types: agricultural · hunter · fisher · river · artisanal · healer · warrior · **spiritual** · messenger · builder · mercantile · integrated foreign. See [[Clan System]].

Unlocks include **sponsoring expeditions** and **administering productive areas**.

### Forms of Government

| Form | Benefits | Risks |
|---|---|---|
| **Council of families** | Greater loyalty · better dispute resolution · lower risk of authoritarianism | Slow decisions · rivalries · smaller clans without influence |
| **Leadership by Prestige** | Better diplomacy · higher morale · easier alliances | **Authority weakens after defeats** · unstable succession · dependence on the leader |
| **Spiritual council** | High Teko · more efficient healing · better control of environmental crises · greater spiritual stability | High ceremonial cost · conflicts between traditions · lower military autonomy |
| **Confederation of villages** | **More indirect population** · better territorial mobilization · lower administrative cost · greater resource diversity | Villages may leave the union · slow military decisions · rivalry between leaders |
| **Warrior leadership** | Fast mobilization · better defense · more efficient campaigns | Internal conflicts · **loss of Teko** · family resistance · attempts to make power permanent |

**Buildings:** great council house, leader's house, assembly, central storehouse, **mediation tribunal**, distribution center, great square · clan seat, clan courtyard, lineage house, own dock · bronze smelter, canoe workshop, **flour house**, great granary, dye house, herb house · **trail network**, wooden bridges, **elevated roads in flooded areas**, docks, rest posts, territorial markers · great barter square, regional trading post, **visitors' house**, artisans' district · fortified palisade, earthen wall, wooden towers, river post, border fortress · **house of stories**, apprentice school, **house of songs**, **navigators' school**, **house of oral maps**.

**Military:** archer, spearman, shield warrior, veteran hunter, forest scout, ceremonial guard, river patroller, military messenger, veteran warrior, rudimentary field engineer.

### Signature formation — Circle of the Village

Units organized around a commander, **spiritual leader** or objective gain defense against flanking, morale, resistance to fear, better protection of support units and **greater efficiency in urban or forest combat**.

Loses efficiency when the center is eliminated, units are dispersed, the group pursues enemies or terrain prevents communication.

### Cultural trait — Network of Trails

**Low-impact** trails granting fast forest movement, food transport, communication, better exploration and warrior mobilization.

**Enemies without territorial knowledge suffer:** reduced speed · **greater ambush risk** · difficulty locating paths.

### Community Alliance System

Possible relations with other villages: alliance · **kinship** · trade · protection · ritual cooperation · military cooperation · rivalry · historical enmity.

Alliances are reinforced by marriages, festivals, food exchange, joint expeditions and shared defense.

**Missions:** form the great council · recognize the clans · **integrate an allied village** · organize the trail network · form a standing army · create a great house of healing · raise a great ceremonial house · **protect the territory** · open a regional route · master iron.

**Advancing:** 100 citizens · clan seats · functioning council · clan missions · **at least one allied village integrated** · trail network · fortified defense · standing army · trade route · sustained spiritual system · iron · avoid internal war · high Teko · **essential natural areas preserved**.

## Iron Age — Regional Dominion of Villages (150 in the capital)

> The territorial network may contain additional population in subordinate villages, provided they are treated as separate settlements.

**New entities:** Tupã · Paranã · Anhangá. Those of Bronze **expire** on turning the era — the choice among the three of Iron is mandatory. Switching to another culture of the same Descent triggers the [[Deities and Passives|Wrath of the Abandoned God]].

### Forms of Government

| Form | Benefits | Risks |
|---|---|---|
| **Confederated council** | High community loyalty · better resource diversity · lower risk of authoritarianism · greater defensive mobilization | Slow decisions · villages may abandon the confederation · **difficulty in prolonged campaigns** |
| **Leadership by Prestige** | Strong diplomacy · higher morale · lower administrative cost · better family relations | Defeats reduce authority · unstable succession · dependence on the leader's personality |
| **Hereditary chieftaincy** | Continuity · more predictable administration · greater centralization | Incapable heirs · rivalries · **rejection by the other villages** · succession crises |
| **Spiritual council** | High Teko · better environmental balance · more efficient healing and rites · greater spiritual protection | High ceremonial cost · warrior resistance · **impractical decisions in military crises** |
| **Warrior confederation** | Strong army · fast mobilization · better territorial defense · efficient expeditions | Militarization · loss of Teko · rivalry between commanders · attempts at permanent centralization |

**Economy:** great barter squares, communal storehouses, river routes, regional trails, **tribute in goods or contributions**, **seasonal fairs**, trade expeditions, specialization between villages, food distribution, crisis reserves.

**Buildings:** great council house, regional assembly, mediation tribunal, central storehouse, **house of oral records**, visitors' house, distribution center · expanded seat, great lineage house, **house of the ancestors**, dock, productive estate · iron mine, primitive blast furnace, **expanded flour house**, canoe workshop, weaving complex, herb house, dye workshop · regional trail network, bridges, elevated roads, river ports, **messenger posts**, territorial markers · **great regional fair**, trading post, iron market, **expedition point**, protected trails · fortified palisade, **wood and earth citadel**, river forts, **hidden posts in the forest** · public wells, house of healing, communal guard, **emergency shelters** · **great house of stories**, healers' school, navigators' school, house of songs, **school of community administration**, **house of oral maps**, medicinal garden, ancestral monuments.

**Military:** veteran archer, iron spearman, shield warrior, veteran hunter, forest scout, leader's guard, ceremonial guard, river patroller, military messenger, field engineer, light siege unit, commander, communal guard.

**Possible special units:** **Guardian of the forest** · **Warrior of thunder** · **Navigator of Paranã** · Spiritual hunter · Campaign healer · **Scout of Anhangá**.

### Signature formation — Defense of the Forest

Units positioned in **forests, rivers or known trails** gain camouflage, evasion, vision, morale, speed and resistance to flanking.

Loses efficiency in completely open areas, devastated terrain, dense enemy cities and **regions without prior knowledge**.

### Cultural trait — Network of Tekoás

The capital may integrate villages and communities, each specialized in agriculture, hunting, fishing, healing, craftsmanship, war, navigation, spirituality or trade.

**Loyal villages:** more food shared · more warriors · more routes opened · **more knowledge transmitted** · greater regional Teko.
**Dissatisfied villages may:** refuse contributions · abandon the confederation · **close trails** · back another leader · form their own alliance · rebel.

### Oral Tradition System

Records events through stories, songs, ceremonies, names, lineages, territorial markers, ritual objects and **the memory of elders**.

**Recorded events grant:** Prestige · morale · legitimacy · bonuses to descendants · **territorial claims** · special traditions.

**The death of memory keepers without successors causes:** loss of knowledge · **forgotten routes** · genealogical disputes · reduced Teko.

### Territorial Mobility

The culture may move part of its production or population when the soil is exhausted, the region suffers drought, there is military pressure, animals disappear or the water is contaminated.

| Planned migration | Forced migration |
|---|---|
| Preserves population · **restores the previous environment** · opens new areas · may create secondary villages | Reduces Teko · disorganizes families · causes resource loss · increases rivalries |

**Missions:** **hold the great gathering** · integrate the network of villages · **preserve the great territory** · modernize agriculture · build the great ceremonial house · create a river fleet · form a regional army · administer the sectors · create a diplomatic network · hold a regional celebration · resolve a clan crisis · resolve a succession crisis · **respond to an environmental threat** · establish regional influence.

**Era risks:** war between clans · **village secession** · succession crisis · authoritarianism · **deforestation** · river contamination · **soil exhaustion** · famine · epidemic · forest fire · invasion · route blockade · **loss of traditions** · **death of memory keepers** · loss of Teko · village rebellion · forced migration · conflict between spiritual and military leaders.
