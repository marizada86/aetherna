# 3-Ability Combat System
**Category:** [[05 - Combat and Factions]]

## The Competitive Standard
Aetherna uses an elevated, tilted tactical camera. Combat is **precision action PvP**: positioning, range, execution timing, stamina and reading intent all matter, but the implementation is designed for latency and multiplayer groups. See [[Visual and Mechanical Direction]].
* **Fixed Loadout:** Each player may equip only 3 normal Abilities and 1 Ultimate at a time.

## The Forging of the Hero (No Fixed Classes)
There are no class selection screens. Abilities are unlocked through work proficiency (profession XP):
* A player who spends hours chopping trees unlocks brute-force axe attacks and bleeding.
* A player who mines gains defense and stun abilities with hammers.
* The system allows hybrid builds, combining abilities from different professions in the same loadout.

The 3 normal abilities are **permanent**: they enter the [[Grimoire]] and belong to the player forever.

## The Divine Ultimate — borrowed, never owned

The fourth slot works under the opposite rule. **The ult belongs to the deity, and the deity belongs to the city.**

A player only has access to an ult while in a city whose patron deity grants that ability. It **does not enter the Grimoire** and does not travel with the character.

| Event | Effect on the ult |
|---|---|
| City advances an Era | The deity expires. **All citizens lose the ult** and gain access to the three of the new deity |
| City switches culture | The same, plus the [[Deities and Passives\|Wrath of the Abandoned God]] on the city |
| Player loses citizenship | Loses the ult immediately |
| Player migrates to another city | Gains the ults of the new city's deity |
| Nomad, with no city | **Has no ult at all** — only the 3 profession abilities |

Within the current era, the citizen chooses **one of the three paths** of the city's deity: Attack, Defense or Mobility. See [[Divine Ult Rules]] and [[Culture System]].

### Design effect

The fourth slot stops being personal progression and becomes **political dependency**. Losing the city means losing combat power. This ties combat to citizenship and gives real weight to the Council's choice of deity — it sets the combat ceiling for the entire population.
